# ordin desktop — User Experience Guidelines

> **Mode:** draft
> **Generated from:** this HLD (revision 0.5.0, revised to 0.27.0); shown on screen in the clickable prototype `docs/design/ordin-desktop/user-interface.html` (12 — UI prototype)
> **Revision:** 0.58.0
> **Last updated:** January 2026

These guidelines turn the design's goals for business users into rules for the people who build ordin's screens and for the agent packages that appear in them. Each rule traces to a goal (G), a decision (ADR) or a sequence elsewhere in this design. Where the design is silent, a rule is marked as a UX assumption (UX-A) and listed at the end for review.

They do not fix component names, command names or the interface framework (OQ-1). Those belong to `/spec`.

## 1. Who we design for

| Audience | Who they are | What they see | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business user** | A person who is not technical, signed in with accounts they already hold. | A conversation with Ordin, and what Ordin shows beside it: agents, tasks, inputs, approvals, results, documents, crews and memory, in plain language. Never a path, a tool name, a version or a command-line message. | G1, G10, ADR-012 |
| **Engineer** | A person working in a workspace that links a code repository (ADR-050; before revision 0.10.7, a person the organisation profile enabled for engineering mode). | Everything the business user sees, plus the fixed engineering views — the workspace's **Code** group in the tree, the code editor, change review, terminal and git — with technical detail shown. *From revision 0.36.0 this row no longer lists a project view: there is none, and the repository is the Code group of its workspace (UX-A18, ADR-047).* | G13, ADR-019, ADR-028, ADR-047 |
| **Support staff** | IT or the platform team, helping a person. | Diagnostics: journal events, versions and technical names. | 02 — Architecture, *Diagnostics* |

**The default reader is the business user.** Every screen outside engineering mode and Diagnostics is designed for them, and is tested with them (§12).

## 2. Principles

| # | Principle | What it means on screen | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | **The person talks to Ordin; the screen fits the task.** | The conversation with Ordin is always in view. The person says what they want; Ordin chooses the agent, task or crew and shows only what this step needs — a form, a list of documents, a set of changes, a plan — assembled from the fixed catalogue of §5.15. What waits for the person stays in the conversation; what is there to look at opens as a screen beside it (UX-A15). The person never has to learn where a feature lives. | ADR-027, ADR-028, ADR-046 |
| P1 | **The application does the technical work.** | No screen asks the person to type, choose or judge anything technical. The person's actions are: sign in, say what they want, choose between options Ordin offers, supply inputs, and approve or reject a stated consequence. Ordin and its agents make every change to a document; the person can still open any file in its own app. | G10, ADR-012, ADR-029 |
| P2 | **Talk or click — both are first-class.** | Talking to Ordin is the default route, and every form, choice and button Ordin shows is an equal way to answer it: the person may type or click, and neither is secondary. A form is something Ordin shows (§5.15), filled in from the conversation for the person to check and complete. Anything that runs a task, changes a document or reaches other people comes back as a form or an approval card and waits for the person (§5.12). A typed "yes" is never an approval. | User decision of January 2026 (decision 10); ADR-027, ADR-028 |
| P3 | **Show consequences before they happen.** | Every externally visible or destructive action is shown — what, where, what it affects — and waits for the person. | G8 |
| P4 | **Report what happened, not what the model thinks.** | Progress shows observable events. No invented completion percentages, no internal reasoning. *From revision 0.13.0 (ADR-068)* a short list of plain steps may sit above the events — from a saved workflow, the crew plan, or the plan an agent states — with "step 2 of 4" counted from that list; a step an agent marks done with no activity seen says so. | ADR-068 |
| P5 | **Nothing is dropped silently.** | When an agent or crew starts, the person sees, in plain words, what it will and will not do. | G9 |
| P6 | **Nothing is lost.** | Every change to a file is a version. Removal goes to the system's bin. A restart never loses a completed step or repeats an external action. | G8, G11, ADR-015 |
| P7 | **One problem, one sentence, one action.** | An error is one plain sentence with at most one action. The application tries a repair before it shows the error. | 07 — Cross-cutting, *Error handling* |
| P8 | **The person stays in charge.** | Crews have a visible stop control; crews never continue on their own after a restart; nothing Ordin or an agent changes leaves the computer — a push to SharePoint, Confluence or GitHub, a post, a commit — until the person approves it. | G4, ADR-021, ADR-029, sequence *Recovery after restart* |
| P9 | **Ordin carries the load and translates.** | Ordin answers what it can for the person: agents' first questions, choices with an obvious answer, technical detail. What reaches the person is short, plain and comes with Ordin's recommendation (§5.16). | ADR-027 |

Rules for writing:

- Use the person's language: "Send to IT", not "Submit diagnostic bundle".
- Name things by what they are to the person: the agent's name, the place's name, the file's name.
- Address the person as "you". Agents are named, not "the system" or "the AI".
- Expand every acronym, or avoid it.
- Write every interface string as a catalogue message, never as literal text (ADR-042). en-GB is the source locale, with British spelling; the person sees their own locale.
- Name the product and the coordinator through the brand strings, never literally (ADR-041). "Ordin" in these guidelines is the default brand's coordinator name.
- Buttons are verbs that say what happens: "Approve and post", "Add agent", "Stop crew". Not "OK", "Submit" or "Yes". A button shown as an icon alone is allowed only on the rows of a tree (UX-A14); its verb is its accessible name and its tooltip, and the node's actions menu shows it in words.

The views of revision 0.7.0 — Start, Inputs, Work, Approvals, Results, Files, Crews and Memory — are no longer places in the navigation. Each becomes something Ordin shows when the conversation needs it, or a screen the person opens from the tree (UX-A15), and the person can ask for any of them in words ("show me this workspace's documents", "what's running?"). This settles the question, open since revision 0.6.0, of whether the navigation could be shorter than the eleven views: it is six parts plus engineering mode. From revision 0.10.13 Home and the search box make eight (ADR-058, ADR-059).

Rules:

- **Every workspace is always visible.** The side rail lists all of the person's workspaces, never folded behind a menu. The open one is marked, and its name and where its documents live are repeated above its screens; the conversation's tabs also switch workspace (§5.13, UX-A15).
- **A connected ordin server is its own group** (revision 0.42.0; UX-A66). Below the person's own workspaces, under the server's name, with its workspaces and their counts. Everything from a server says "On [server]" (§5.31).
- **Switching is one click and switches everything.** Choosing another workspace changes the conversation and the screens shown, because each workspace keeps its own (§5.12, §5.13). Work started in one workspace goes on reporting there after the person switches away, and *Needs you* says which workspace each item belongs to; choosing an item from another workspace opens that workspace first.
- **Approvals are reachable from everywhere.** A pending approval shows a count on the *Needs you* tab and on its workspace's conversation tab, and it opens as a screen in its workspace, with a one-line card in the conversation that caused it (UX-A53). The *Needs you* tab lists every waiting item grouped by workspace, the open workspace first; choosing one shows that workspace's conversation or screen with the item, its heading focused (UX-A37). A message that arrives while *Needs you* is shown marks its conversation tab as new. A running task waiting for a decision says so where it is shown.
- **Home and the search box decide nothing (UX-A29, UX-A30).** Every row on Home and every result in the search box opens the item in its own workspace, switching to it first, as *Needs you* does. Neither carries a button that approves, sends, posts or publishes; an action opens the same screen, form or card as asking Ordin would (P2). What the search box finds in another workspace is shown to the person only; an agent uses it only once the person adds it to the agent's workspace (§5.13).
- **Nothing important lives only in the scroll.** Anything still waiting for the person — an approval, a question, a form — is also in *Needs you*, so scrolling the conversation never hides it.
- **The conversation can be shortened without losing anything (UX-A12).** Every item Ordin shows that no longer needs the person carries **Close**, which folds it to one line, "[its title] · closed", with **Show again**. A decided approval card closes the same way and keeps its decision on the line. **Close finished items**, in the workspace's actions menu (⋯), folds every such item in the open workspace at once, and **Show closed items (N)**, above the conversation while any are closed, reopens them. Asking in words ("tidy up") does the same (P2). Above the conversation there is no permanent button for it: the tools there are **Summarise**, **Clear** and **Earlier**, which keep the panel uncluttered (UX-A21). Closing never deletes: the item, its history and its versions stay. An item still waiting for the person — an unanswered question, a form not yet sent, an undecided approval card, an offer not yet answered ("Save as workflow?"), a crew plan not yet started, work still running — has no **Close** until it stops waiting.
- **The workspace tree can be folded and pruned (UX-A13).** **Collapse all** on the Workspaces heading folds the tree to workspace names, keeping each one's count of what waits for the person; **Expand** reverses it, and the arrow on any node shows or hides what is in it. **Remove workspace…** is among each workspace's actions (UX-A14); it opens the same confirmation as in §5.13 without opening the workspace first. Removing is also asked of Ordin in words.
- **The person can always reach a thing without asking (UX-A11).** Under each workspace, the tree holds:
  - **Documents**, grouped by where they live, then each linked location — SharePoint library or folder, Confluence page tree, repository folder, folder on this computer — by its own name, with its sub-folders and documents and the number of documents it holds.
  - **Agents**, each with its tasks.
  - **Workflows**, when there are any, with **Import workflow…** on the group (ADR-064, §5.20).
  - **Dashboards**, when there are any (ADR-067, §5.21).
  - **Email**, when the workspace links email, with each email link and its count of new messages (ADR-070, §5.22).
  - **Telegram**, when the workspace links a Telegram group or channel, with each linked chat and its count of new messages (ADR-075, §5.25).
  - **Running now**, while work runs, with each item's state (§8).
  - **Knowledge**, with its number of pages (§5.10, ADR-057).
  - **What Ordin remembers**.
  - **Earlier conversations**, when there are any, each by its first request, with its date (UX-A21).

  Choosing a location or a document opens the documents view of §5.6 as a screen, at that location or with that document selected; the same screen also opens from **Documents** above the screens. It opens without a message from Ordin, because looking is not a request (P2, UX-A15). On its screen, the documents view leaves its own tree out, because the side rail carries it. Dropping a folder on the tree adds it to the open workspace (§5.6).
- **Every node has its own icon and its own actions (UX-A14).**
  - **Icons.** Each kind of node has a distinct icon shape: workspace, each place documents live, folder, page tree, each document kind, agent, agent that answers elsewhere, task, workflow, running work, memory. Colour may tint an icon but never carries the meaning alone, and the kind and any state are part of the node's accessible name (§10). Icons are generic shapes, not other companies' logos (ADR-041).
  - **Actions.** A node's main action shows on its row when the pointer is over it, when it has keyboard focus or when it is selected, and always on touch screens. Every other action is in the node's actions menu, opened from **⋯**, by right-click, or with Shift+F10 or the Menu key. A workspace's row shows only **⋯**, so its count stays in view.
  - **What actions do.** Actions are the same ones the person could ask Ordin for, and do what asking would (P2): "Ask Ordin about this", "Open in [app]", "Start…" on a task, "Talk to [agent]", "Run…", "Duplicate…" and "Export…" on a workflow, "Stop crew…" on running work, "Remove from workspace". No tree action approves, sends, posts or publishes. **"…" means more comes before anything happens**: an action ending in "…" opens something to fill in, choose or confirm first — a form, a crew plan, a rename, a confirmation ("Remove Release planner…", "Stop crew…") or the application's approval card ("Send back…", "Publish to Confluence…") (§5.5, §5.15). An action without "…" happens at once ("Talk to Supplier analyst", "Open").
  - **Other workspaces.** Using a node in another workspace opens that workspace first, as *Needs you* does. Unfolding another workspace with its arrow only looks, and does not switch.
  - **Large folders.** Folders list their documents 25 at a time with **Show more**, so a folder of thousands stays usable (A-21).
- **Looking opens a screen; the conversation stays a conversation (UX-A15).**
  - **Choosing never writes.** Choosing something in the tree, or **Open** on a card, opens it as a screen in the centre of the window. It adds no message and does not move the conversation's scroll. Focus moves to the screen's heading.
  - **Screens are tabs.** Each workspace has its own row of screen tabs. Something already open comes to the front instead of opening twice. Up to six tabs stay open; a seventh closes the oldest one that is neither in front nor showing running work. Closing a tab never deletes anything. *Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).* The row of tabs scrolls inside its own width; tabs out of view are listed under **More tabs (N)** at its end, so the window never scrolls sideways, even at 200 % zoom.
  - **The conversation has its own panel**, with one tab per workspace the person has opened, a mark for new messages and a count of what waits for the person. Choosing a conversation tab switches the whole window to that workspace, so the screens and the conversation in view always belong to the same workspace. *From revision 0.36.0,* while more than one workspace's tab is open, each has a close control, "Close tab · the conversation is kept"; closing it removes only the tab, and closing the open workspace's tab switches the window to the next tab's workspace. The *Needs you* tab never closes (UX-A37).
  - **What stays in the conversation**: questions with choices and offers that wait for an answer, in full, because they wait for the person (§5.12). **Approval cards open as screens (UX-A53)**, the same way as forms. **Forms and crew plans open as screens (UX-A35)**: at once, with the cursor in them, and with a card in the conversation that says **Waiting for you** and cannot be closed until the form is sent or the crew started; *Needs you* lists them too. Documents, previews, changes, plan and progress, results and memory appear as a one-line card with **Open**, and in full on their screen (§5.15).
  - **Every decision waits in *Needs you* (UX-A36).** Each approval card, question with choices, form, crew plan and offer that waits has an entry in *Needs you* until it is decided. Opening one from *Needs you* puts focus on its heading, never on one of its buttons (UX-A2).
  - **A decision Ordin asks for is a question with choices (UX-A36)**, with Ordin's recommendation and its reason, never a message with loose buttons: "Pricing notes changed in Confluence", "A new version of Release planner", "Add it to this workspace?".
  - **Adding is always a screen (UX-A33).** Adding documents, a code repository or an agent happens on **Add to this workspace** or the agent library in the centre, whether the person started it from the tree, a button or words. The conversation carries one line saying what Ordin opened and found, never an add button. The one exception is a file dropped on the conversation: Ordin asks, as a question with choices, whether to add it or use it once (§5.13).
  - **Ordin does not move the person's place.** A screen comes to the front at once only when Ordin answers what the person has just said or done. Results that arrive later open as a new tab marked *new*, without coming to the front. Every screen and every conversation keeps its own scroll position.
  - **Narrow windows.** Where there is no room for three columns, the conversation slides over from the side, opened from **Conversation** in the header, which shows a mark when something new arrives.
- **Editing fills the centre (UX-A59).** Every editing page fills the whole central pane below the screen tabs: a Markdown document being edited (with its toolbar, or as text with **Show Markdown**), a code file, a knowledge page and an email draft. One bar sits above the editor:
  - on the left, the editing tools: **Save**, **Cancel** (or **Discard my edits** in the code editor), the save status and, where the page has them, the formatting buttons;
  - on the right, the page's other actions: **Ask Ordin about this**, **Review changes (N)** while an agent's changes wait, and **Show as a document** for a Markdown file in *Code*;
  - everything else, such as where it lives, the repository and branch, the language server's line, notes, sources and keyboard help, is behind **Details**, which opens a small panel above the editor.

  An email draft keeps To and Subject above the message. A Markdown document's reading view uses the same full-pane layout (§5.6): **Edit**, **Show Markdown** (only in a workspace that links code) and **Publish a copy…** on the left; **Ask Ordin about this** and **Open in editor** (when the file is in a linked repository) on the right; where it lives and its **Published as** list behind **Published as (N) · Details**, or **Details** when it has no copies. Other reading views do not change. In a narrow window (700 pixels wide or less) the editor keeps at least 60% of the window's height and scrolls.
- **The side panels can be resized (UX-A16).** The edges of the side rail and of the conversation panel can be dragged wider or narrower, within set limits. With keyboard focus on an edge, the arrow keys resize it, Home and End go to the limits, and Enter restores the default; a double-click also restores it. The width is remembered on that computer only.
- **Code appears when a workspace links a code repository (ADR-050).** There is no coding mode or setting. Adding a code repository — from **Add documents** ("A code repository"), the workspace's actions menu, or by asking — gives that workspace a **Code** group in its tree, and the engineering views open as its screens (§5.11, UX-A18). Removing the repository, from the Code group's actions menu, removes the group and closes those screens. A workspace without one never shows anything of code. The organisation profile can still keep people from linking code repositories (ADR-019).
- **A workspace with code chooses what the tree shows (UX-A19).** Above the tree, **Show: Documents · Code** switches the *Documents* and *Code* groups on and off, with the header's **Documents** button. One of the two always stays on. Hiding is only a view: anything waiting for the person stays in *Needs you* and in *Changes waiting*. The choice is remembered on that computer only. The switches appear only while the open workspace has code.
- **Back from Home and Settings.** While Home or Settings fills the centre, the side rail shows **Back to your workspace**, which returns to the open workspace's screens and conversation as they were. It is hidden while a workspace is shown.
- **Diagnostics is never linked from an error as the way forward.** The way forward is the error's one action (P7).

## 5. Patterns

### 5.1 Getting ready

Source: sequence *First start*, Setup Assistant.

- The person's only required action is signing in to the agent service the organisation uses, in that service's own page in the system browser (ADR-044). If the person is already signed in, as with Claude Code on a computer that has it, nothing is asked. The button names the service in plain words ("Sign in to Claude"), and the application never asks for a password or key itself, except where a service has no browser sign-in: the company's mail server (§5.22), where the form says so above the password field, and a Telegram bot the person makes (§5.25).
- Show setup as a short list of steps the application is doing ("Getting you ready… 3 of 4 done"), each ticking off as it completes. The count reflects real steps, not an estimate (P4).
- Sign-ins to the company identity provider and to Slack are asked for later, the first time an agent needs them, with the reason: "Release planner needs you to sign in to Slack to ask the support agent."
- **Say what leaves the computer (UX-A70).** Under the steps: "To do the work, Ordin sends what it reads to [Claude or Copilot]. Your documents stay where they live.", then a fold, **What Ordin reads and sends — read the terms**, holding ordin's terms of reference. **Settings › About** opens the same terms as a screen at any time. The terms say, in plain words: what Ordin reads; what it sends to the agent service to do the work, and under whose terms; what stays on this computer; what goes elsewhere only when the person approves a card; how long things are kept and how to remove them; and that ordin is open source, under the Apache License 2.0, with no warranty. Cards do not repeat the terms; their last line is "Nothing is sent or changed outside ordin until you choose." (§5.5). *Added in revision 0.44.0 by the user's decision of January 2026.*
- End in the conversation with Ordin, which introduces itself in two sentences and offers three things it can do now, drawn from the agents the organisation recommends. Getting ready is the one fixed screen a business user sees before the conversation.
- Failures use the setup messages of §6. "No Claude access" offers "Ask IT for Claude access", and, where the company uses Copilot, "No Copilot seat" offers "Ask IT for Copilot access" (ADR-038).
- **No administrator rights, ever (UX-A46, ADR-072).** Getting ready, updates and repairs never show an administrator password prompt, and no screen asks the person to install anything themselves. When the company's settings stop a program from starting, the message names it in plain words and says what is off because of it: "Your company's settings stopped PhotoCraft from opening on this computer. You can still use everything else; images open in your usual app until IT allows it." The action is **Send to IT**, whose card shows the program's name and publisher (and, under *Details for IT*, its hash). The rest of ordin carries on. Where a harness can only be installed by IT, the message is "Claude Code needs to be installed by IT on this computer", with **Send to IT**.

### 5.2 Adding an agent

Source: sequence *Add an agent*, ADR-013, ADR-032, ADR-086, ADR-087.

- **Ordin proposes; the person says "Add it" on the library screen (UX-A33).** When no installed agent can do what the person asked, Ordin searches the agent library — every agent repository the person has (UX-A56) — and opens it as a screen with the best match chosen and marked **Ordin suggests this one**, its detail showing what it does, the tasks it brings and who publishes it, with **Add it**. In the conversation Ordin says one line: what it found, a second match if any ("Contract reviewer is there too, if you want a one-page summary instead"), and what it will do once the agent is added. There is no add button in the conversation.
- The person can also ask directly ("is there an agent for contract reviews?"); the answer is the same screen.
- **The person can browse the library (UX-A17).** **+** on *Agents* in the tree, or a proposal from Ordin, opens the company's agent library as a screen (UX-A15):
  - **Finding.** A search by what the person needs done ("contracts"), a choice of area (Legal, Finance, Procurement and the others the library holds), a choice of agent repository (**All agent repositories**, or one by its display name), and **Only agents I can add**. The number of matches is announced as it changes. Agents Ordin suggests for the open workspace come first, marked "Suggested for this workspace".
  - **Each agent** shows its plain name, what it does in one sentence, who publishes it, its area and **"From: [display name]"** of its agent repository (ADR-086), and, when it applies, "Added to this workspace" or "Needs IT approval", each with a label and an icon.
  - **Choosing an agent** shows the tasks it brings, what it will and won't do, where it runs ("Runs on this computer", "Answers in Slack") and whether the company has approved it, with one action: **Add it**, **Ask IT**, or **Talk to [agent]** once added; and **Ask Ordin about this agent**. *Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).* The one action sits beside the agent's name at the top of its detail, so it is in view at any window size. Above the list, the library shows **Make an agent…** and one **More** menu holding **Import…** and, only for people who can send a review, **Make a part…**, **Make a lifecycle…**, **Parts of an agent repository** and **Add an agent repository…**. *From revision 0.39.0 (ADR-093) also **What it's made of** (§5.29).*
  - **Adding** follows the rules below: "Adding [agent]…", then one line in the conversation saying what it can and cannot do. The agent appears under *Agents* in the tree. The library screen never shows a version, package name, install step, repository address or format.
  - **Two agents with the same name** from two agent repositories are both listed, each with its own "From:" line.
  - **Both formats look the same (ADR-087).** An agent from an APM-format repository is listed, chosen and added exactly as one from an ordin-format repository: same detail, same **Add it**, same checks. The format is shown only on the repository, in Settings › Agent repositories, as "ordin format" or "APM format". An agent whose parts do not all carry across into ordin is not offered; the reason goes to Diagnostics.
  - Agents for code appear only in workspaces that link a code repository (ADR-050). When nothing matches, the screen says so and offers **Ask Ordin**. *From revision 0.25.0 it also offers **Make an agent…**, which the screen always has too (one label from revision 0.35.0, UI-D48). The person's own draft is shown above the list with its state, and an agent they published is marked **Made by you** (§5.26). In a workspace that links code, **Import an agent package…** is there too.*
- Ordin never adds an agent without **Add it**, and never removes one.
- There is no install wizard, version picker or permissions step. While adding, Ordin says "Adding Contract reviewer…"; on success it says what the agent can and cannot do, in plain words, and carries on with the original request.
- *From revision 0.31.0 (ADR-089).* **What an agent works with.** When an agent leaves some of its working practices open (which language, which testing approach), Ordin picks them from the workspace's code, otherwise uses what the agent's maker chose. Choosing the agent shows the result as one line, "Uses: Go, Test pyramid" (and, from revision 0.34.0, its language and style where the agent offers a choice, ADR-090), with **Change**, which lists only what the agent allows. Nothing is asked before **Add it**. An agent that cannot be set up for this workspace is shown as unavailable with one sentence ("Needs a project in a language it supports"), never with a pack name or class. *From revision 0.33.0 (decision 64).* A choice the maker left to the agent is asked by the agent the first time its work needs it, as a question with choices whose options come from ordin, each in plain words ("Many small tests, a few end-to-end ones"); the answer then shows on the agent's line in the workspace, with **Change**.
- An agent outside the organisation's trust policy is not offered as addable: Ordin says "There's an agent for this, but it needs IT approval first" and opens it in the library, where its one action is **Ask IT**. Never offer the person a way to trust it themselves.
- A failed safety check: "This agent could not be added safely" with **Send to IT**.
- **Updates.** An update that does not change what the agent may do or read is applied between runs without a question, and the agent's line says so: "Updated between runs today; what it may do hasn't changed, so nothing was asked." An update that changes what the agent may do or read reaches *Needs you* as "A new version of Release planner is available" with **what changed** in plain words, and the person chooses (A-16). *Amended in revision 0.36.0: until then the rule followed the version number (updates within the same major version applied without a prompt); the prototype's rule, what the agent may do or read, replaces it.*
- *From revision 0.25.0 (ADR-082).* **A withdrawn agent** stops at once in every workspace. Ordin says in one message who withdrew it, why, and that what it did is kept, with **Remove [agent]…** and **Find another agent for [what it did]**. Its entry in the tree says "Withdrawn by its publisher — no longer runs", and *Needs you* lists it until the person chooses.
- *From revision 0.34.0 (ADR-090).* **A withdrawn part.** When something an agent is built from is withdrawn, the agent stops as a withdrawn agent does, and Ordin says which of its abilities was withdrawn, in plain words. Where a replacement exists and the agent passes ordin's checks with it, Ordin offers it as an update under the usual rules.
- *From revision 0.34.0 (ADR-090).* **What an agent never does** is enforced by ordin: when an agent tries something it is not allowed, the step is refused and the conversation says so in one line ("Contract reviewer isn't allowed to publish code, so ordin stopped it").
- Removing an agent is asked of Ordin or done from the workspace's agent list. It changes only this computer, so it uses a confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*, UX-A57), not an approval card: "Remove Release planner?", with "Its working files are deleted. Your documents are not touched.", **Remove** and **Cancel**.

**Agent repositories (UX-A56, ADR-086).** The agent library is drawn from one or more agent repositories, each shown by its display name.

- **Where they are listed.** Settings › Agent repositories lists each one with its display name, where it is ("on GitHub"), its format ("ordin format" or "APM format") and how many agents it offers. *Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).* The format is shown only in a workspace that links code. The address field of **Add an agent repository…** shows "For example https://github.com/northwind/finance-agents" under it, and says as it is typed when the text can't be a GitHub repository. Those the company names carry "Set by your company" and cannot be removed. Its address is never shown after it is added. *From revision 0.37.0 (ADR-091),* a repository the person can send a review to also has **Show its parts** (§5.27), and the agent library has **Parts of an agent repository** beside **Make an agent…**; people who cannot send a review to any agent repository see neither.
- **Adding one.** **Add an agent repository…**, in Settings › Agent repositories and on the agent library screen, opens a short screen:
  1. One field: "Paste the repository's web address".
  2. ordin checks it with the person's sign-in for that place (the sign-in is asked for first, with the reason, when it is missing) and says in one line what it found and what the person can do with it: "Found 12 agents in APM format. You can read it; publishing needs permission from its owners." A repository the person cannot read says so in one sentence, and nothing is added.
  3. A name field, filled with the repository's own name, for the person to keep or change: this is the display name shown everywhere afterwards.
  4. **Add** keeps it; its agents appear in the library with its name on their "From:" line.

  Adding a repository never lets an agent run anything the company has not allowed: its agents go through the same checks and the same IT approval as any other (ADR-013).
- **When the company manages them.** When the company's settings turn adding off, Settings and the library say "Your company manages the agent repositories. Ask IT to add one." with **Send to IT**, which shows what will be sent before sending (§6).
- **Removing one the person added.** **Remove [display name]…** uses a confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*): "Remove [display name]?", with "Agents you added from it stay, but stop receiving updates." The library then marks those agents so.

### 5.3 Starting a task

Source: sequence *Run a task with one local agent*; ADR-027.

- **A task starts by asking Ordin.** The person says what they want in their own words. Ordin chooses the agent and task, or forms a crew (§5.7), and says in one line who will do it: "Supplier analyst will compare the three quotes."
- When the task needs inputs, Ordin opens a **form** as a screen in the centre (UX-A35), built from the task's arguments (§11): labelled fields, document pickers and choices, already filled from the conversation and the workspace. Ordin says so in one line ("I've opened the form beside us and filled in what I could"). The person checks it and presses the task's own verb ("Compare quotes"). Typing the missing answers in the conversation is equally valid and updates the form (P2). Documents can be dragged onto its fields from the documents screen or the tree.
- When the request is clear and complete, and starting the task changes nothing outside the workspace, Ordin starts it without a form and says so (UX-A8): "Started — Supplier analyst is comparing the three quotes. Stop".
- When the request is ambiguous, Ordin asks one question with choices, never several questions at once.
- Validate before the run starts. Say what is missing in the field that needs it, or in Ordin's one question.
- Documents are given by naming them, by dragging them onto the conversation, or by picking from the workspace. Granting access is part of the same action: "Give these files to Release planner (it can read them)."
- Before the first step, show **"What this agent will and won't do"** — the start-up report in plain words — collapsed to one line when everything is carried across, expanded when something is not (P5).

### 5.4 Showing progress

Source: 07 — Cross-cutting NFRs.

- Show the **current step**, completed checks, missing inputs and blockers, as they happen.
- Each line is an observable event: "Read supplier responses (3 files)", "Waiting for your approval", "Asked the support agent in Slack".
- **No progress bars with invented percentages.** A count is allowed only when the total is known ("Round 3 of at most 10").
- **Three layers (ADR-068, UX-A42).** Every progress screen reads top to bottom:
  1. **Now** — one sentence with the step count: "Now: comparing the Q3 rates — step 2 of 4". When the person is needed it leads with that: "Waiting for you: approve sending the summary", with **Review**. Beside it: "Running for 6 min", and for remote agents "last checked 20 seconds ago".
  2. **Steps** — three to seven steps in plain words, each with a symbol and a word: ✓ **done**, ● **now**, ○ **next**, – **skipped**. Steps come from the saved workflow, the crew plan, or the plan the agent states; a round Ordin adds reads *added by Ordin*. When the plan changes, one line says so ("Plan changed: one step added") and the count follows.
  3. **What happened** — the observable events of each step, folded under it, opened with **Show what happened**. Real counts from tools appear here: "Read 12 of 40 quotes", or "12 so far" when the total is not known.
- **What is claimed and what was seen.** A step an agent marked done with no activity recorded during it reads **Done — the agent reports no activity for this step**, in the same neutral style as other lines; it is not an error.
- **No steps known.** When no plan is available, the screen shows Now and What happened only.
- **The same words everywhere.** Home's *Running now*, the one-line card in the conversation, notifications and the screen tab's name for screen readers use the Now sentence and the step count.
- **No model reasoning** on screen. A concise summary of what was done is enough.
- Remote agents through wrightrun send no partial output (wrightrun A-33). Show "Working on it in [agent's home]… last checked 20 seconds ago", not a spinner with no information.
- The person can always see how to stop a run, and what stopping does ("Stop — files written so far are kept").
- **Finished work can be run again (UX-A31).** A finished task or crew run shows **Run again…** on its progress screen. It opens the same form or crew plan, filled with the previous inputs; an input with a newer version uses the newer one and says so ("Quote — Kestrel.pdf: newer version since last time"). Nothing starts until the person presses the form's button or **Start** (§5.17). A run the person stopped does not offer **Run again…** or **Every week…**; asking Ordin in words still starts the work again.

### 5.5 Approvals

Source: G8; Approval Service.

**Where it appears (UX-A53, ADR-080).** An approval card opens as a screen in the centre of its workspace, at once and with focus on its heading, like a form. The conversation that caused it keeps a one-line card: its title, "Check it and press “[Verb]”" and **Waiting for you** with **Open**; once decided, the line says what was decided ("Approved: Send back."), and **Close** on the screen closes its tab. *Needs you* lists it until decided. The card itself is unchanged: built by ordin from the action, never by an agent, and decided only by its buttons. *Until revision 0.24.0 the card appeared in full in the conversation.*

Every approval card has the same anatomy:

> **[Verb] [object]?** — a question in plain words
> *From ordin — built from the action, not written by an agent*
> **Where:** the destination (a place, a Slack channel, a company system)
> **Affects:** the records or files touched, by name and version
> **Changes:** what will be different afterwards
>
> **See [what it holds] · [Verb] · Reject** — the first button names what it shows: "See the changes", "See the page", "See the files", "See the command", "See what will be sent"; a card where nothing more can be shown has none. Where the person may decide later, **Reject** reads **Not now**.
> *Nothing is sent or changed outside ordin until you choose.* — the card's last line

The line under the question and the last line are the same on every card, in the application's fixed wording; no agent or tone setting changes them (§5.19).

Worked example in this anatomy:

> **Publish the revised account brief?**
> Where: Sales workspace
> Affects: Account brief, version 4
> Changes: Updated revenue table and three action items
>
> **See the changes · Publish · Reject**

Rules:

- The approve button repeats the verb ("Publish", "Approve and post"). It is never the visually default action for externally visible or destructive steps (UX-A2).
- Show the exact content that will leave the computer — the message text, the file — behind the card's first button, "See what will be sent" (or "See the files", "See the page"). *Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).* A message or a command is shown open on the card itself: a command in full, in a code block, with where it runs. Only a message longer than 12 lines folds, showing its first 6 lines and **Show the whole message**; until it is opened, the verb reads "[Verb] — you've read the start".
- *Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).* A document held back because it changed where it lives carries Ordin's recommendation, first and marked: "I recommend: Ask Ordin to redo the change on the new version — [name]'s edit is kept and yours is added." 
- Never approve by timeout. A pending approval waits, and survives a restart.
- After a rejection, say what happens next: "Rejected. Release planner has been told and will continue without posting."
- Posting in Slack as the person always says so: "Post this in #ordin-agents **as you**? Everyone in the channel will see the question and the answer." (ADR-008, ADR-009)
- Group approvals only when they share one destination and one consequence; otherwise one card per action. **The one exception is sending changed documents back** (ADR-029): the changes Ordin made for one request share one card, listed by origin, because the person asked for them together (§5.6).
- **The application builds every approval card from the action itself.** Ordin and agents can ask for an approval; they cannot write one, and they cannot show anything that looks like one (§5.15).
- An approval keeps its place in *Needs you*, and its one-line card in the conversation says it waits, until decided, and Ordin does not move on to unrelated work in that conversation as if it had been approved.

**Confirmations (UX-A57, decision 58).** An action that changes only this computer, and sends nothing anywhere, uses a confirmation, not an approval card. These are: **Stop crew**, **Remove [agent]…**, **Remove workspace…**, **Remove [agent repository]…**, **Remove…** on a workflow (§5.20), **Discard** on an agent draft, **Clear** for the conversation, **Remove [dashboard]…** ("Remove the dashboard “[name]”?", §5.21), **Discard** on an email draft ("Discard this draft?", §5.22), **Move to Bin…** on a file on this computer (§5.6), and removing a linked place from a workspace: a code repository, a documents location, an email link or a Telegram link ("Remove “[name]” from this workspace?", §5.6, §5.11). A new action that changes only this computer joins the list. *This amends ADR-080, which kept "Clear this conversation?" in the conversation. The four entries after **Clear** were added in revision 0.36.0, as the prototype shows them.* *From revision 0.37.0 (ADR-091) the list also holds **Discard** on a part draft (§5.27).*

**Sign out of email** and **Remove Telegram**, in Settings, act at once with no confirmation, as the prototype shows: each says in one line what it did and that the workspaces keep their links ("Signed out of email, and your password is removed from this computer's keychain. Workspaces keep their email links; nothing is read until you sign in again.").

- **What it looks like.** A small dialog in the centre of the window, over what is there:
  - its title is the question: "Remove workspace?", "Stop crew?", "Clear this conversation?";
  - one or two lines say what goes with it and what stays ("Its links, conversation and history are removed. Your documents stay where they are.");
  - two buttons: the verb ("Remove", "Stop crew", "Discard", "Clear") and **Cancel**. For a verb that removes, discards or stops, the verb is never the default button, so Enter does not choose it (UX-A2).
- **Keys and focus.** Focus moves to the dialog's heading when it opens, and Tab stays inside it. Escape cancels, as **Cancel** does. On closing, by either button or by Escape, focus returns to the control that opened it.
- **What it never has.** No *Where*, *Affects* or *Changes* lines, no "From ordin" line, and no entry in *Needs you*: a confirmation is answered at once or cancelled, and it waits for nothing.
- **What it is not for.** Anything that leaves the computer, or changes something at its origin, uses an approval card, never a confirmation.

### 5.6 Documents, results and sending changes back

Source: G11, G14, ADR-015, ADR-025, ADR-029, File Service, Craft Toolkit.

**Showing documents.** Ordin shows a workspace's documents when the person asks, when a task needs a choice of documents, or after a change. It opens as a screen, and the conversation carries a one-line card for it (§4, UX-A15).

- **Built for many files and deep folders** (A-21):
  - The documents view has two parts: a **tree** of everything linked to the workspace, grouped by where it lives (SharePoint, Confluence, GitHub, this computer), and a **list** of the open folder or page tree. A document's details are on its own page, below (UX-A52).
  - The list has sortable columns — name, kind, changed, changed by — and a toggle to a grid of previews for image-heavy folders. It loads in pages and renders only visible rows, so a folder of thousands of files opens at once.
  - A **breadcrumb trail** shows where the person is, starting with the origin's name; each step is a link.
  - **Search** covers names and document text across everything linked to the workspace. Filters for where it lives and kind are behind **Filter**, so the view starts with one box. A *State* column shows only when a document in the list has a state; **Who can use [folder]** is behind a heading. While content is still being read it says how much is covered ("Searched 41,200 of 58,000 documents").
  - Several documents can be selected (click, Shift-click, Ctrl/Cmd-click) and given to Ordin together ("Ask Ordin about these 4").
- Asking in words is equal to browsing: "find last year's supplier contract" returns the same view, narrowed to the matches.
- **The document page (UX-A52, ADR-079).** Choosing a document in the tree, or its name in the list, opens a screen for that document only. A click elsewhere on a row selects it, for **Ask Ordin about these**. The page shows more as the person asks for it:
  1. *At a glance*: one sentence with its kind, where it lives and its **origin of change** ("Word document in SharePoint, changed today at 10:40 by R. Ahmed."); its state, when it has one ("Changed by Ordin — not yet sent back", with **Send back…**); **New since you last looked**, while it is; **Open in [app]** and **Ask Ordin about this**.
  2. *On request*, behind headings: **Versions** and **Where it lives**, with **Show the folder**.
  3. *In a menu* (⋯): **Who can use [folder]** and **Remove from workspace**.
- **Changed marks (UX-A52).** A document someone changed where it lives since the person last opened it has a blue dot in the tree and in the list; each folder above it shows a count while folded. The dot goes when the person opens the document. It uses the accent colour, never amber, and its row's accessible name says "changed since you last looked". *Changed since you looked*, at the top of *Documents*, lists only those documents.

**Markdown documents (UX-A20, ADR-048).** Markdown is a first-class kind of document. Much documentation lives in Markdown, often in a repository, and is published to SharePoint and Confluence as copies; some documentation lives only in SharePoint or Confluence, and stays the original there.

- **It reads as a document.** Choosing a Markdown document opens it as a formatted page on its own screen — headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, emphasis and links — never as Markdown text. Outside workspaces that link a code repository, Markdown symbols are never shown, in the page or in the changes (see **Show Markdown** below).
- **The page fills the centre (UX-A59).** The formatted page opens on the same full-pane layout as its editor. One bar above it holds **Edit**, **Show Markdown** (only in a workspace that links a code repository) and **Publish a copy…** on the left, and **Ask Ordin about this** and **Open in editor** (when the file is in a linked repository) on the right. Where it lives and the **Published as** list are behind **Published as (N) · Details**, or **Details** when there are no copies. Anything waiting for the person sits above the page, which fills the rest of the pane: a suggestion from a changed copy (**Bring it in** / **Leave it**), a copy that cannot come back, and "Changed by you — not yet sent back" with **Send back…** (below). *From revision 0.36.0.*
- **Every way in leads to the formatted page**: the tree, a document list Ordin shows in the conversation, its name, double-click or Enter on a row of the documents view, and a Markdown file in the *Code* group.
- **Friendly editing.** **Edit** turns the page into an editor with a toolbar: Heading, Bold, Italic, Bulleted list and Numbered list. The editor fills the central pane, with **Save**, **Cancel** and the toolbar in the bar above it (§4, UX-A59). **Save** keeps the change on this computer, marked "Changed by you — not yet sent back". *Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).* The editor's bar also has **Send back…**, which saves the edits first and says so on the card: "Includes your edits from [time], saved just now."  A paragraph, list or table the person did not touch keeps its exact original text, so the change shown for review contains only what they changed.
- **In a workspace that links a code repository** the page also offers **Show Markdown**, which edits the same document as text, and the changes shown there include Markdown symbols. Elsewhere nobody sees Markdown symbols. *Amended in revision 0.36.0: this said "people who code"; since ADR-050 and UX-A22 it depends on the workspace, not the person.*
- **Published copies.** The original lists its copies under **Published as**, each by name and where it lives, each opening that copy. A copy is marked "Published copy of [original]" in the tree and the documents view, with **Open the original** as its first action. A copy is not edited as the original.
- **One approval for the original and its copies.** Sending a changed Markdown original back uses one approval card. Its **Affects** line lists the original and every copy, and its **Changes** line says each copy is remade from the original on this computer, and by which app: Confluence pages by ordin, Word copies by WordCraft, PDFs by PdfCraft, slides by DeckCraft (ADR-048).
- **A change made in a copy comes back as a suggestion.** When someone changes a copy at its origin, the original shows "[person] changed the [origin] copy … Bring the change into the original?" with the change in plain words, **Bring it in** and **Leave it**. Leaving it means the next update of the copy replaces their change, and that update's card says so. A copy is never written over silently.
- **Publish a copy…** on a Markdown document asks where the copy should go — a Confluence page, or a Word document, a PDF or slides in SharePoint — and opens an approval card naming the destination and the app that makes the copy. For slides, each heading becomes a slide. When the workspace has no location of that kind, Ordin opens **Add to this workspace** at that place instead and says so: "This workspace has no [place] location yet. I've opened Add to this workspace at [place]; add one there and I'll publish the copy to it." (§5.13).
- **The first publish card (UX-A43, ADR-069)** names, in its **Where** line, the destination and who can see it there ("SharePoint › Procurement › Rates — Procurement team, 42 people"); in **Changes**, the format and the app that makes it, and, for a repository original, "follows main in supplier-portal". It carries one choice, ticked: **Keep this copy up to date when rates.md changes**, with the sentence "Changes that reach GitHub update this copy without asking again. Changes by Ordin or an agent still ask you first." A file of the same name at the destination is named, with **Use another name**.
- **Copies follow the original.** A copy kept up to date is remade when a change reaches the original's origin — a change merged on GitHub or GitLab, a version the person saved in a local folder, a new version in SharePoint or Confluence — and its line under **Published as** says "Updated from GitHub, 10:15 · Sam Lee's change". Home's *Since you last looked* groups these ("3 copies of rates.md updated from GitHub"), and each updated copy carries the changed mark in the tree (ADR-079); nothing is written into the conversation. A change still on this computer never updates a copy by itself.
- **Each copy says what it is.** The top of every copy reads "Published copy of rates.md, from [place], branch [name]. Last updated [date, time]. Make changes in the original." (§5.6; ADR-069).
- **A copy that cannot come back.** A PDF or slides copy changed at its destination cannot be read back into Markdown. The original says "[person] replaced the PDF copy in SharePoint. ordin cannot bring changes back from a PDF." with **Open** and **Replace it at the next update**; until the person chooses, that copy is not updated.
- **A copy held for a wider audience.** If more people can see a copy's destination than the card said, its next update waits in *Needs you* on a card that names the change ("Now visible to everyone in the company, was Procurement team").
- **Each copy's own actions**, from its line under **Published as**: **Open**, **Update now**, **Stop keeping up to date** (or **Keep up to date**), **Remove copy…**, which asks before deleting it at the destination.
- **The original says when it was last checked** when it lives in a repository: "Last checked GitHub at 10:15", with **Check now** when a check failed.
- **New text documents are Markdown.** When Ordin or an agent writes a new text document, it is written as Markdown on this computer, and published as a copy where the readers are.

**Changing documents.** By hand, inside ordin, the person edits only four things: Markdown documents, with the toolbar (above, ADR-048); knowledge pages (§5.10, ADR-057); agents' own memory pages, with the same toolbar (§5.10); and email drafts, with the same toolbar (§5.22, ADR-070). Everything else is changed by asking Ordin, which has an agent make the change (P1, ADR-029), or by the person in the document's own app. *Amended in revision 0.36.0: this said only Markdown could be edited by hand; knowledge pages, memory pages and email drafts were already editable in ADR-057, ADR-070 and the prototype.*

What each kind of document allows (G3-2, revision 0.36.0). Where neither the prototype nor the design says, the cell reads *not decided*.

- The person says what should change ("update the pricing table in the supplier summary from the new quotes"). Ordin has the right agent make the change through the document's app, as the table says, or as text for Markdown. *Before revision 0.36.0 this bullet also listed GridCraft for spreadsheets; the prototype shows no agent changing an existing spreadsheet.*
- When done, Ordin shows **the changes**: for text, the changed passages; for Word, PowerPoint and PDF, before-and-after pages; for images, before and after side by side. One line per document says what changed.
- The person can ask for more changes in words, or **Undo** one document's change, before anything is sent back.
- **Open in [app]** stays available on every document, for the person to read or work on it in its own app. A change the person makes there is their own and follows the rules of its origin.

**Sending changes back.** Every change Ordin made goes back to its origin only after the person approves (P8):

- One approval card per request, listing every changed document grouped by where it lives:

  > **Send 3 changes back?**
  > Where: SharePoint — Procurement › Suppliers (2 documents); GitHub — billing-docs (1 document)
  > Affects: Supplier summary.docx, Pricing.pptx, pricing.md
  > Changes: Pricing table updated from the Q3 quotes; summary slide rewritten; one paragraph corrected
  >
  > **See the changes · Send back · Not now**

- Each document in the card can be unticked, so the person can send some changes back and keep others.
- Sending to GitHub says, in the **Changes** line, that the change will be saved to the shared copy for everyone working on it. In engineering mode the card also shows the branch and commit message.
- **A Markdown original goes back with its copies** in one approval card (UX-A20).
- **One way back for each file (UX-A18).** When the workspace's **Code** group holds the repository a document lives in, that document is left out of the approval card, and the card's **Changes** line says it goes back with the engineer's commit. Otherwise it goes back through the card, as above.
- **Not now** keeps the changes on the computer, marked "not yet sent back", and the request stays in *Needs you*.
- **Changed at its origin meanwhile.** If someone changed the document at its origin since Ordin read it, nothing is sent for that document. The card says "Supplier summary was changed in SharePoint by Sam Lee at 13:40, after Ordin read it", and offers **See both versions**, **Ask Ordin to redo the change on the new version** or **Keep theirs and drop Ordin's change**. Overwriting the origin is never offered.

**Other rules.**

- Removing a document from a workspace removes the link, never the document. Removing a file on this computer says "Move to Bin". The word "delete" is reserved for removing an agent's working files (§5.2).
- **Move to Bin…** changes only this computer, so it uses a confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*, UX-A57): "Move “[name]” to the Bin?", with "It leaves this workspace and goes to this computer's Bin, where you can still get it back.", **Move to Bin** and **Cancel**.
- **Remove from workspace…** on a linked location (a SharePoint, Confluence, GitHub or local folder linked to the workspace) uses a confirmation too: "Remove “[name]” from this workspace?", with "Your documents stay where they are. Agents in this workspace can no longer use them.", **Remove** and **Cancel** (UX-A57). **Remove from workspace** on a single document acts at once, and Ordin says "Removed “[name]” from this workspace. The document itself is untouched where it lives."
- No app for a file kind: "No editor for this kind of file — open it in your usual app?", with **Open in your usual app**.
- Word and PowerPoint files that the ArtCraft apps cannot yet change (ADR-018, C-13): Ordin can read and summarise them, and says plainly that it cannot change them yet, while **Open in your usual app** stays available. The refusal is about one document at a time, not the whole format: "I can read and summarise Borealis framework.docx, but I can't change this one yet: WordCraft can't edit some of what it contains. Other Word documents I can change."
- For each folder or origin, the workspace shows which agents may use it and how ("Release planner can read and change"). Changing access is a choice of three, not a settings page: *can't use*, *can read* or *can read and change*, in this workspace only. *Amended in revision 0.36.0: this said "a toggle"; the prototype offers the three choices.*
- **Drag and drop works everywhere a document can go**: onto the conversation (give it to Ordin, and offer to add it to the workspace), onto the workspace name (add a link to it), onto a form (give it to the task). While dragging, the drop target says what will happen ("Add Supplier quotes to this workspace"). Dragging out saves a copy.
- A refused protected location: "This folder belongs to the system and can't be added."

### 5.7 Crews

Source: G4, ADR-006, ADR-027, Crew Orchestrator, sequence *Crew run*.

- **Ordin forms crews; the person does not build them.** When a request needs several agents, Ordin opens a **crew plan** as a screen (UX-A35): the purpose in one line, each agent with what it will do, the limits and the estimated use (§5.18). One action, **Start**; the person can ask for changes in words ("leave out the pricing agent", "leave the slides out"), and the plan on the screen changes. There is no crew builder.
- Limits are shown as plain sentences with their defaults: "Stop after 10 rounds", "Stop after 60 minutes", "Ask me if the same agent fails twice". The person can lower a limit, never raise one past the company's maximum, and the control shows the maximum. *Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).* The limits fold into one line, "Limits (company defaults) · Estimated use: Medium", until the person opens it; inside, the time limit comes first, and rounds are explained: "Stop after 10 rounds — a round is one step by one agent".
- A running crew shows each round: which agent Ordin handed work to, with the brief in one line, and the result. Hand-offs refused by a limit are shown with the reason. When the crew starts, Ordin opens its progress as a screen and says so (§4, UX-A15).
- **Stop crew** is visible at all times while a crew runs. Its confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*), "Stop crew?", states the consequence, including that agents running elsewhere cannot be cancelled and their answers will not be used.
- Questions from crew members go to Ordin first (§5.16). When the crew stops on a limit or Ordin needs the person, the crew view leads with **the question to the person**, then the state so far.
- **Two ways a crew is coordinated (ADR-064).** The crew plan says which, in its first line under the purpose: **"Same steps as last time"**, when it follows a workflow, with its numbered steps; or **"Ordin decides each step as it goes"**, for a new crew, with each agent and what it will do. A workflow's crew plan names the workflow and its version, and links to it with **Open the workflow**.
- **"Save as workflow".** After a crew Ordin coordinated finishes, Ordin offers **Save as workflow** with a suggested name. **Save as workflow…** opens the workflow screen (§5.20) with the steps Ordin condensed from the rounds, marked *Not saved yet*, with **Save** and **Discard**. A workflow can be asked for by name later ("run the supplier rates refresh on the Q4 quotes"); Ordin shows its crew plan before starting. *Before revision 0.11.0 this was **Keep this crew**.*
- **When the steps do not fit.** If a step fails, a check finds a shortfall, an agent is missing or changed, or an input is missing, Ordin says in one line that it has taken over and from which step ("Step 3 failed twice, so I've taken over from there"). The progress screen marks each step Ordin changed as **Changed by Ordin**. When the crew finishes, Ordin offers **Update the workflow…**, which opens the workflow screen showing the steps before and after; nothing changes until the person saves.
- After a restart, a crew shows "Paused while ordin was closed" with **Continue**. It never continues on its own.

### 5.8 Agents that run elsewhere

Source: G5, ADR-008, ADR-009, Slack and wrightrun adapters.

- Ordin uses remote agents as it uses local ones, and they appear beside local ones in the workspace's agent list. A small note says where they answer, only when it changes what the person experiences ("Answers in Slack — the channel can see the conversation").
- Features a route lacks are stated before the run, not discovered during it: "This agent can't be stopped once asked." "This agent shows its answer when it's finished."
- **No talking directly.** An agent that answers elsewhere has no **Talk to [agent]** in the tree or the agent list; the person reaches it through Ordin (§5.12).
- **Its progress** reads "Now: the support agent is working on it in Slack — last checked 20 seconds ago" and has no **Stop**, because the route cannot cancel a question once asked; the line "can't be stopped once asked" is shown from the start (§5.4).
- No reply: "No answer from the support agent yet" with **Open the conversation in Slack**.
- An approval the remote agent asks for in Slack: "The support agent is waiting for your decision in Slack" with **Open in Slack**. It has an entry in *Needs you*, "Support agent waits for you in Slack", until the person has decided in Slack.
- *Amended in revision 0.43.0 (ADR-097, ADR-098; UX-A67).* A **hosted ordin agent** reached through its caller interface shows live progress and has **Stop**, because the route can follow and cancel a run. The two lines above about polling and "can't be stopped once asked" apply to the Slack route only. Reaching a hosted agent asks for the person's company sign-in the first time (A-17), and never shows a pairing code. Where the person is one of its approvers, its cards appear in *Needs you* as "Support agent asks: [action]", with who asked, and are decided there. Where the person is one of its operators, the hosted agent also appears under **Hosted agents** in the side rail, with its runs, waiting approvals, recurring duties, settings and knowledge entries. *From revision 0.43.2 that screen shows first whether the agent is running, what waits for an approver, today's runs with who started each, and its repeating work. Who can reach it, settings by channel, knowledge entries and what it doesn't carry across are folded (UX-A64). What people said to the agent is not shown. **Erase what it keeps about a person…** states what it covers and what it doesn't before anything is erased. Settings › Servers refuses a hosted agent with where to find it instead.* A person without the role sees "You don't have access to [agent]. Ask IT if you need it."
- *Amended in revision 0.61.0 (ADR-113; UX-A80).* On the operator screen of a **knowledge agent**, a **Knowledge** section: each repository with "Read up to [date]" or "Can't be read just now", documents read today and "[n] documents wait for tomorrow's reading budget", glossary conflicts in one sentence each ("BRL is a name in both [glossary 1] and [glossary 2]; [glossary 1] is used."), the front doors and proxies it talks to, whether it keeps a sources record and for how long, and whether outside tools may search it.

### 5.9 Recovery after a restart

Source: sequence *Recovery after restart*.

- On start, every interrupted item is in a known state, and the person is told once, in one summary: "While ordin was closed: 1 task finished, 1 task was interrupted, 1 crew is paused."
- An **interrupted** run keeps its partial results and offers **Try again**.
- An external action with an **unknown outcome** is shown with what was sent and where, and the person decides. It is never resent automatically.
  - The summary names it ("…and I'm not sure one message was posted"), and *Needs you* lists it as "One message may not have been posted".
  - **Review what was sent** shows the message, where and when it was sent, and Ordin's recommendation ("Check the channel first — sending again could post it twice."), with three choices: **It arrived — mark as done**, **Send it again**, which comes back as an approval card first (§5.5), and **Open in Slack**.

### 5.10 Memory and knowledge

Source: ADR-030; ADR-111, UX-A79.

- **What Ordin remembers is visible.** The person can ask "what do you remember about this workspace?" or open it from Settings. Ordin shows the facts as plain sentences ("You prefer pricing tables in euros"), each with where it came from (a conversation, a document, an agent) and when.
- Each fact has **Correct** and **Forget**. Forgetting is immediate and says so: "Forgotten. Ordin won't use this again."
- Facts belong to the workspace they were learned in. Ordin does not use them in another workspace unless the person says so: with **Remember everywhere** on the fact, or in words ("remember this everywhere"). The fact then says "All workspaces".
- **Open Knowledge**, at the foot of *What Ordin remembers*, opens the workspace's Knowledge screen (below).
- When an answer relies on something remembered, Ordin says so in a few words ("as you asked last week, in euros"), so the person can correct it.
- A fact that may be out of date is marked "may be out of date" rather than used silently.
- Each agent's own memory pages (from its package) are shown in *What Ordin remembers*, under *Agents' own pages*, as one line each with **Open** and **Remove page**, saying which agent keeps the page and when it last changed. **Open** shows the page on its own screen, read and edited like a Markdown document (UX-A59). They are the agent's working notes, unlike the read-only pages that came with it (*From agents*, below).

*Amended in revision 0.60.0 (ADR-111, UX-A79):* the person sees the whole memory, things and links included, as lists.

- **Two tabs.** *What Ordin remembers* has **Facts** (first) and **Things**. Agents' own pages, **Open Knowledge** and the team knowledge lines stay under both.
- **Each fact** says what kind it is, in words — *Something that happened*, *Something known*, *A summary* — and "True since 12 Oct" (or "True from … to …" for one no longer true). A corrected fact says "Replaced "[the old statement]"". **History**, in the fact's menu, lists every version oldest first with when each was true, and the links each version stated. A summary says "Made from 6 facts"; opening it lists those facts, and one no longer true is marked so.
- **Fading.** A fact that happened and has not been used for a long while says "Fading — not used since [date]" with **Keep**. **Keep** makes it something known, and it no longer fades: "Kept. Ordin will keep using this." Fading is a sign of disuse; it never says that a fact will be deleted.
- **Things.** A search box, "Find a thing", and one row per thing: its name, "Also called …", "12 facts · 4 links", and "Explained in [page]" with **Open the page** when a knowledge page's title or other name matches. A thing opens to its facts and its links, each link in words: "Borealis supplies packaging · since 3 Oct", with who stated it ("Remembered by Supplier analyst", "Set by you", "From the page …"). The other thing's name opens that thing. Things never appear as a drawn diagram in this revision.
- **On a link:** **Correct** (the two things and the words between them, then **Save**) and **Forget** ("Forget that Borealis supplies packaging? The facts stay."). A link no longer true is shown with its dates and no actions.
- **On a thing:** **Forget everything about this…** asks first with the numbers — "Forget everything about Borealis? This forgets 12 facts and 4 links." — with **Cancel** in focus and **Forget all of it**. If something changed meanwhile, the dialog says so and shows the new numbers. **Same as…** asks which thing this one is the same as; **Make them one** joins them, and this name becomes another name of the one chosen.
- **Forgetting a fact** that summaries were made from says so before it happens: "This also forgets 1 summary made from it."
- **What Ordin used.** Under an answer that used memory, a quiet link **What Ordin used** opens a panel with the facts and links recall gave Ordin for that answer, each with **Correct** and **Forget**. A fact from a knowledge page shows "[page] › [section]" with **Open the page**. Items forgotten since are counted at the foot. Ordin still says in words when it relies on something remembered.
- **Remembered during this run.** A run's progress, under *What happened*, lists each fact remembered during it with "Remembered by [agent]" or "Remembered by Ordin".
- **Hosted agents.** On the operator screen of a hosted agent (UX-A67), the agent's owners see **What [agent] remembers**, one section per place — "In #channel", "In direct chats with [person]", "Everywhere this agent works" — with the same tabs. People in a channel see only what one answer used, by asking the agent "What did you use?" or with its **What I used** button where the channel has buttons.
- **Never shown:** entity, relationship, graph, node, edge, episodic, semantic, consolidated, salience, decay, embedding, score (§3).
*Amended in revision 0.61.0 (ADR-113, UX-A80):* knowledge from knowledge agents.

- **Adding one.** Settings › Knowledge agents › **Add a knowledge agent…**, or saying "connect this workspace to Procurement knowledge" to Ordin: the address (or a name the company listed), then **Sign in with your company account**. Before **Add**, one paragraph says what it means: "Ordin will ask Procurement knowledge when this workspace's own memory isn't enough. Only your question is sent — never the conversation, your files or what Ordin remembers here." and, from the agent's details, "Procurement knowledge keeps a record of which sources each answer used, for 365 days. It never records your question or who asked." or "Procurement knowledge keeps no record of answers."
- **Details.** The agent's name, who it serves in plain words ("People in procurement-staff"), how many repositories it reads and "as of [date]", the record sentence above, and **Remove**. A front door the company set says *Set by your company*; removing it asks once and it is not added again.
- **Look wider.** Ordin asks a knowledge agent by itself when its own memory is not enough. The person can always say "look wider", and Ordin does.
- **What Ordin used.** A fact from a knowledge agent shows the agent's name, the repository and file ("procurement/policies › suppliers.md"), the section, "version of [date]" and "as of [date]", with **Open the source** where the person can read it. A fact from a page shows the page's title and section. When the agent keeps a record, the panel ends with "Answer reference: [short id]" and **Copy**. Knowledge-agent facts have no **Correct** or **Forget**: they are the agent's, and change when the repository changes.
- **More found.** Results that arrive after the answer appear under it as "More found · 3 facts", with the facts in **What Ordin used**. A source that did not answer says "[agent] did not answer in time." No banner, no sound.
- **Refused.** "You aren't in the group Procurement knowledge serves." or "Sign in again to ask Procurement knowledge." in one line under the answer; the answer from the workspace's own memory stands.


**Knowledge** (ADR-057, UX-A28). Memory is small facts; knowledge is pages that explain.

- **Where it is.** Each workspace has **Knowledge** in the tree, above What Ordin remembers, with the number of pages. A page that may be out of date is announced on the tree item ("a page may be out of date"). The screen lists pages on the left, in two groups: **This workspace** and **From agents**. A search box filters them. The chosen page shows on the right.
- **What a page shows.** Who wrote it and when ("Written by Ordin · 9 Oct", "· edited by you"); the page itself, formatted as any Markdown document; and **Where this comes from**: each source with where it lives and the version read. A source can also be **Our conversation**, with its date, or **Came with an agent**, naming the agent. A source that changed since is marked "changed since".
- **Out of date.** A page whose source changed says so at the top, naming who changed what and when, with **Bring up to date** and **Leave it**. Bring up to date shows the lines that would change, as a before-and-after, with **Use the update** and **Keep the page as it is**. The page never changes without the person's choice.
- **Actions.** **Edit** (as a Markdown document, saved on the computer only), **Ask Ordin about this**, **Publish to Confluence…** (opens an approval card; the page stays the original), **Remove page** (with Undo).
- **From agents.** Pages that came with an agent are read-only and say so: "Came with the Supplier analyst agent. Read-only: it changes when the agent is updated." Each page is labelled with the agent only, never with its version or package name (decision 56, which amends ADR-057; §3).
- **Ordin says when it writes.** When work finishes and Ordin writes or updates a page, it says so in one line, naming the page, with **Open the page**. When an answer comes from a page, Ordin names the page, and says if the page may be out of date.
- **Search.** Documents search lists matching knowledge pages above the documents, as "Knowledge pages — Ordin's summaries, with their sources".
- **Links** (*added in revision 0.52.0, ADR-104*). A page's links to other pages work as links. Under **Where this comes from**, **Pages that link here** lists every page that links to this one. A link to a page that does not exist is shown muted, "No page called …", and starts working when such a page is written. Renaming a page updates the links in other pages at once and says so: "Links on 3 other pages were updated to the new title."
- **Other names and tags.** Under the title: "Also called …" and the page's tags. **Edit** has a field for other names, one per line, and one for tags. The list's filter box takes words and `tag:`, `state:out-of-date`, `written-by:ordin` or `written-by:person`.
- **Suggestions.** Ordin may suggest a link ("Link "supplier rates" to Supplier rates?", **Link it**, **Dismiss**) or say "This page and … may cover the same subject" (**Open**, **Dismiss**). Nothing changes until the person chooses.
- **Editing in another app.** The workspace's knowledge folder can be opened in another Markdown app. What it adds to a page is kept; a note created there becomes a page; a page renamed or deleted there is renamed or removed here, with Undo. Files in the folder that cannot be pages are listed under the page list.
- **Empty.** A workspace with no pages explains what will appear there and how to ask for one.
- **Type and description** (*added in revision 0.61.0, ADR-112, UX-A80*). Under the title: the page's **Type** (*Explanation*, *How-to*, *Decision*, *Reference* or *Glossary*) and its one-sentence **Description**. **Edit** has a picker for the type beside the tags and a one-line field for the description. A type another app wrote is shown as written. Marks at the top, in the same place as "may be out of date": **Draft** ("This page is a draft."), **Deprecated** ("This page is no longer used; Ordin uses it only when nothing else answers."), and "This page may be out of date" when the date its author set for checking it has passed. The person's own edits mark the page as checked by them; nothing more is shown. `index.md` and `log.md` in the knowledge folder are never listed as pages.

### 5.11 Engineering mode

Source: G13, ADR-019 to ADR-022, ADR-047, ADR-050, ADR-052 to ADR-054.

*From revision 0.10.7, engineering mode is not a setting or a mode the person turns on: it is what a workspace shows once it links a code repository (ADR-050). "Engineering mode" below means "a workspace with code".*

Engineering differs from the business view only where an engineer needs it: exact detail, a decision on each change, their own commit, push and terminal, and room to keep an editor and a terminal open. Everything else — the tree, screens as tabs, the conversation panel, approval cards, *Needs you*, agents, crews and memory — is the same (UX-A18).

- Inside engineering mode, technical detail is shown: paths, diffs, branches and command output.
- **Show: Documents · Code** above the tree lets a person who codes hide either group (§4, UX-A19).
- **A repository document is both a document and a file.** Its document screen says "also [path] in Code" and offers **Open in editor**; its file offers **Show as a document**. An edit saved on the document screen joins *Changes waiting* as the coder's own change and goes in the next commit. After the push, its published copies follow the branch they were made from (ADR-069): a push to that branch updates the copies kept up to date and says so in the conversation, and an approval card offers to update the others; a push to another branch updates none, and says the copies follow when the change is merged.
- **Code is part of a workspace.** A repository is something a workspace links to, as it links SharePoint, Confluence or a folder. Once a workspace links a code repository, its tree has a **Code** group after *Documents*, holding the repository by name with its branch, and the number of changes waiting for review. There is no separate project view and no second tree.
- **The Code group is the side-rail tree of §4 (UX-A14)**, with the same rows, folding, keyboard use, icons and actions menu. It shows technical names: file and folder names as they are, and each file's and folder's number of changes waiting. A file's actions include "Open in editor", "Review changes" when changes wait, "Show as a document" when the file is also one of the workspace's documents, "Show history" and "Copy path". The group's actions are "Review changes", "Open terminal" and "Check the code against the documents".
- **The engineering views are screens (UX-A15).** The code editor (one tab per file), **Changes waiting** and the terminal open as tabs in the centre, like any other screen. They are always the same fixed views, never generated (ADR-028). The conversation stays beside them.
- **Each agent works in its own copy (UX-A24).** A coding task works in its own copy of the repository, on its own branch, never in the engineer's working copy. Its declared checks run there without asking; any other command asks first. Only the changes the engineer accepts reach the working copy.
- **Two attempts, keep one (UX-A24).** Asked to "try two ways", Ordin starts one task per attempt. *Changes waiting* shows them side by side — the request, the branch, whether the tests pass, new problems and the change itself — each with **Keep this one**, and Ordin's recommendation in the conversation. Keeping one brings its changes in for review and removes the others' copies.
- **Code files are edited with completion (UX-A27).** A code file opens editable, unless business users also read it as a document, in which case it is edited on its document screen. Completion comes from the file's language server as the engineer types, after `.` or `::`, and on Ctrl+Space: a list under the caret with each item's kind, name and signature, and the server's name; arrows choose, Enter or Tab inserts, Escape closes. Tab inserts a tab; Escape then Tab leaves the editor. **Save** (⌘S or Ctrl+S) writes to the working copy, and *Changes waiting* lists the file as the engineer's own for the next commit; **Discard my edits** returns to the saved file. A line says where completion comes from, or that it is off.
- **Every language is highlighted (UX-A26).** The editor and the diffs colour each file by its language, with or without a language server. A line names the language and its server with its state ("Go · gopls · Running"), or says "highlighted · no language server" with **Language servers…**, which opens Settings › Code. A file of an unknown kind shows as plain text.
- **The code editor fills the centre (UX-A59).** **Save**, **Discard my edits** and the save status sit on the left of one bar above the editor; **Ask Ordin about this**, **Review changes (N)** and, for a Markdown file, **Show as a document** on the right. The repository and branch, the language server's line and the completion line are behind **Details**. *From revision 0.36.0; until then those lines sat above and under the editor.*
- **Language servers in Settings (UX-A26).** Settings › Code lists each language with its server, pinned version, where it comes from and where it runs, with a choice of server where the company approves more than one, **On**, **Restart**, *Server settings* as JSON with **Save** (invalid JSON is refused, saying so), and **Add a language** from the company's list. Nothing outside that list can be entered.
- **Each change shows what the language server found (UX-A25):** "✓ No new problems" or "⚠ 1 new problem: [the problem]", and each task shows whether its tests pass.
- **Open in [IDE] (UX-A25).** The Code group, its repository and each file have one **Open in [IDE]** for each IDE found on the computer — **Open in IntelliJ IDEA**, **Open in VS Code** — and Settings › Code › *Open code in* chooses which appear. It opens the IDE and writes nothing to the conversation; a short notice confirms it. Edits made there show in ordin as the engineer's own.
- **Changes waiting** lists everything in the workspace that waits to go back, in two parts:
  - *Documents · go back after you approve*: documents Ordin changed, with **Send back…**, which opens the approval card as a screen (§5.5, §5.6).
  - *Code in [repository] · goes back with your commit*: the agents' changes, grouped by task under the agent's name, request, branch, test outcome and new problems, one entry per file and hunk, each with **Accept** and **Reject**. A rejected entry is dropped; the working copy is untouched.
- **Each change says why.** Every code change names the document it is based on — "Based on Supplier rates Q3.xlsx", with where it lives and **Open** — or the agent's own page in *What Ordin remembers*. The proposed commit message may cite it.
- Commit and push are the engineer's buttons only, below the code changes. **Commit** is available once every change waiting has been decided. An agent may fill in the proposed commit message, marked as proposed.
- **Commit, Push and Show history run in the engineer's terminal.** Each writes its git command and the command's output into the terminal and opens it, so the engineer sees exactly what ran (`git commit`, `git push`, `git log` for one file). They are the engineer's own actions, not an agent's, so ADR-022's rule that agents never write to the terminal still holds. *Amended in revision 0.49.0 (`/spec`):* Commit, Push and Show history are performed by ordin (in-process git) and ordin writes the equivalent git command and its outcome into the engineer's terminal, marked as ordin's; no shell runs them.
- **A file the business users also read is shown on both sides.** Documentation in the linked repository is a document under *Documents* for business users, by its plain name, and a file under *Code* for engineers; its change entry says so. It goes back only with the engineer's commit (§5.6).
- **Agents work across documents and code.** The person can ask, in words or from the Code group, whether the code matches the workspace's documents. The answer is a result listing each file, what it follows or contradicts, and **Review changes** or **Ask for a change**; a change asked for joins *Changes waiting*.
- A check command that needs approval uses the approval card of §5.5, with the command shown. Its outcome comes back in the conversation, with the command output behind "Show the steps behind this"; it never appears in the engineer's terminal (ADR-022).
- When the engineer and an agent changed the same file, including in the engineer's own IDE, change review shows both, and the entry offers **Keep yours** and **Use the agent's** instead of **Accept** and **Reject**. The agent's version is applied only when the engineer chooses **Use the agent's**; it is never applied over the engineer's change on its own.
- **Removing the repository.** **Remove [repository] from this workspace…**, on the Code group, uses a confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*, UX-A57): "Remove “[repository]” from this workspace?", with "Your documents stay where they are. Agents in this workspace can no longer use them.", **Remove** and **Cancel**. It then removes the Code group and closes its screens (§4).
- **An agent draft's files (UX-A54).** In a workspace that links code, an agent draft (§5.26) also shows **The agent's files, written by ordin**: the files generated and formatted by ordin from the draft, never typed by an agent. The publish card's **See the files** shows the same files (§5.26).

### 5.12 Conversation

Source: P0, P2; user decision of January 2026 (decision 10); ADR-027.

- **The conversation is with Ordin**, one per workspace, in its own panel that is always in view (UX-A15). Every task, crew, document and decision can start there.
- **Talking directly to one agent stays possible** when the person asks ("let me talk to Release planner"). The conversation then shows "Talking to Release planner" with **Back to Ordin**; Ordin keeps track of what was agreed. Crew members are reached through Ordin.
- **The conversation knows what the person is looking at.** When the person points at a document, task, crew or result, the message carries it as its topic, shown as a removable "About: [item]" label.
- **Free questions get plain answers.** Asking about results, progress or why a choice was made needs no form. An answer that points at a result attaches the result (preview, version, where it lives).
- **Requests to act return as forms, plans or approval cards.** A request for work comes back as the task's form (§5.3) or a crew plan (§5.7), ending in the task's own verb. Anything externally visible or destructive uses the approval card of §5.5. A typed "yes" is never an approval.
- **Missing access is shown, not assumed.** If the request needs a folder or origin the agent cannot use, Ordin says so in plain words with the access toggle of §5.6, a tick box in its message: "[agent] can read and change [folder]". Ticking it grants that access at once; the person's tick is the decision, and nothing goes to *Needs you*. The conversation never grants access by itself. *From revision 0.36.1 this is the stated exception to UX-A36, decided by the user on January 2026 ("follow prototype").*
- **The person can steer work in progress.** "Tell Supplier analyst to ignore the late quote" reaches the running task, and the agent takes it into account at its next step. Ordin confirms in one line.
- **Remote agents keep their warning.** Before Ordin asks an agent that answers in Slack, the person sees that they post as themselves and the channel sees the conversation (§5.5, ADR-008, ADR-009).
- **Conversation follows the same words as the screens.** No tool names, paths or model reasoning (§3, P4). In engineering mode, a reply may expand to show the steps behind it.
- **The person can speak instead of typing.** A microphone button in the message box starts dictation; the words appear in the box as they are recognised, and the person edits and sends them. Dictation never sends a message and never approves anything. The first use asks for microphone permission with the reason. When the person's language has no recogniser on the computer, the button says so (ADR-043). *From revision 0.22.0 (ADR-078) on macOS 26 and later only; on Windows and older macOS the microphone button is not shown, and Settings says "Dictation isn't available on Windows" (or "…on this version of macOS").*
- **Ordin speaks the person's language.** Ordin replies in the person's locale. An agent's answer in another language is shown as written, with "Translate" (ADR-042).
- **Ordin is brief.** Replies lead with the answer or the next decision, in a few sentences. Detail is one click away, never in front.
- **Long conversations can be summarised or started fresh (UX-A21, ADR-049).** Every message makes Ordin reread the conversation, on the person's own plan; folding items with **Close** tidies the window but saves nothing.
  - **Summarise**, first of the conversation's tools, has Ordin work from a summary of the conversation instead of the whole of it. The earlier messages fold under "Earlier messages (N) — summarised", still readable; Ordin's message carries **Show the summary**. Anything still waiting for the person stays in place, outside the fold.
  - **Ordin also summarises by itself** when a conversation becomes too long to carry, and says so in one line: "I summarised our earlier conversation to keep going", with **Show the summary**.
  - **Clear** begins a new conversation for the workspace; the conversation cleared is kept, not deleted. It first asks "Clear this conversation?" in a confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*, UX-A57), not an approval card, because nothing leaves the computer. *Until revision 0.27.0 the question appeared in the conversation itself.* The confirmation says in one or two lines what Ordin forgets and what is kept, and offers **Keep the key points first**: points Ordin proposes, ticked by default, saved to *What Ordin remembers*. Its buttons are **Clear** and **Cancel**.
  - **Clearing and summarising delete nothing.** The conversation started over moves to **Earlier conversations**, a screen that lists only each conversation's first request, date and number of messages. Choosing one opens it, read-only, on its own tab, named by its first request, with the line "[date] · [N] messages · kept to read; Ordin no longer works from it." **Ask Ordin about it** and, from revision 0.57.0, **Continue this conversation**. Its node in the tree offers **Read it**, **Ask Ordin about it** and **Continue this conversation**. Ordin reads an earlier conversation only when the person asks about it this way or in words, or continues it; it never draws on one by itself. The new conversation starts with only the line "New conversation · started [time]"; Ordin adds no message. Earlier conversations are removed only by how long the person keeps history (below).
  - **An earlier conversation can be continued (UX-A76, ADR-108).** **Continue this conversation** first asks "Continue this conversation?" in a confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*, UX-A57), with "Ordin stops working from the current conversation and works from “[name]” instead. Both are kept, and nothing is deleted.", **Keep the key points first**, **Continue** and **Cancel**. When the current conversation has no messages, nothing is asked. The current conversation then joins **Earlier conversations**, and the one chosen becomes live: its earlier messages fold under "Continued [time] · earlier messages (N)" with **Show**, and Ordin works from them. Items still waiting move into it; where they were, each shows "Moved to the current conversation". While a crew Ordin leads is running, it waits, as **Clear** does, and says "Waits until the crew finishes".
  - **The conversation picker (UX-A76).** The conversation panel's header names the live conversation — its first request, or "New conversation" before the first message — as a drop-down. It lists the live conversation, the earlier conversations newest first, each by first request and date (at most 20, then "All earlier conversations (N)"), and **Clear**. Choosing an earlier one opens it to read on its own tab, with **Continue this conversation**. It follows the menu-button pattern of §10: arrow keys, Home and End, Escape returns to the button, and each entry's date is announced. On narrow windows it is in the sliding conversation panel.
  - **How long conversations are kept (UX-A58, decision 57).** Settings › History reads "Keep conversations and history for [90] days": one box for a whole number of days, 90 by default, 1 at least. Anything else — empty, 0, a fraction, a word — is refused in place, with one plain line under the box: "Enter a whole number of days, 1 or more." The earlier value stays until a valid one is entered. Under the box: "This is the only way earlier conversations are removed." Conversations, earlier conversations and history older than that number of days are removed from this computer; an approval still waiting and work still running are never removed (A-08). The setting changes only this computer.
  - **Earlier conversations are always one step away**: the **Earlier** button in the conversation's tools (with their number; its accessible name is "Earlier conversations"), the conversation picker (from revision 0.57.0), the *Earlier conversations* group in the workspace's tree (one node per conversation, opening it directly), the workspace's actions menu, and asking in words ("show me the earlier conversations"). Choosing one in another workspace's tree opens that workspace first. Questions, forms, approval cards and other items still waiting move into the new conversation, which opens with "New conversation · started [time]".
  - **Clear waits while a crew Ordin leads is running**, because Ordin coordinates it; the button says so, and asking explains it. **Summarise** stays available.
  - **A quiet nudge.** When a conversation is long, a line above the message box says "This conversation is long — summarise or clear it to keep Ordin quick", with both actions. It never interrupts.
  - **Asking in words does the same:** "summarise this", "clear the chat", "start fresh".
  - **People who code** also see how much of Ordin's context the conversation uses, and the before-and-after figures behind a summary.
  - The same controls apply while talking directly to one agent.

### 5.13 Workspaces

Source: G14, ADR-025, ADR-029.

- **A workspace is a set of links to where documents live**: SharePoint sites, libraries, folders or files; Confluence spaces, page trees or pages; folders of GitHub repositories on this computer; and other folders on this computer. Documents stay where they are; ordin reads them, shows them, and sends Ordin's changes back after approval (§5.6).
- **Creating a workspace is one sentence.** "Create a workspace for the Q3 supplier review" or **New workspace** with a name. Ordin then opens **Add to this workspace** beside the conversation and says so: "I've opened Add to this workspace beside us: tell me where its documents live, or choose there."
- **Adding a link is asked for in words or by dropping.** "Add the supplier folder from SharePoint" — Ordin finds it and opens **Add to this workspace** at that place, with its best match first and marked **Best match**; the person chooses **Add** there (UX-A33). Ordin's line in the conversation says what it found and what is left out ("Its Archive folder isn't included unless you ask"). Dropping a folder or file on the workspace adds a link to it where it is; nothing is copied. Dropping one on the conversation gives it to Ordin, which asks in the conversation whether to add it to the workspace or use it for this question only; that question stays in the conversation, because the person may want it read once (the user's decision, January 2026, UX-A33).
- **Started from the tree or a button, adding is a screen (UX-A23).** **Add documents** (on the *Documents* group, the workspace's actions menu or the search box) and **Add a code repository** open **Add to this workspace** in the centre, like the agent library, and add nothing to the conversation. The screen has four steps, each with **Back**:
  1. *Where it lives*: SharePoint, Confluence, GitHub, GitLab, this computer, a code repository, **Email** (§5.22) or **Telegram** (§5.25; on the desktop only when the Telegram app is on the computer), each with one line saying what it holds and "Linked" when already linked. *Amended in revision 0.54.0 (ADR-106): also **Jira issue**, on the desktop only, "A ticket with its discussion and attachments".*
  2. *Sign in*, only the first time that place needs it, with the reason; the browser opens.
  3. *What to add*: the locations or repositories the person can reach, each with **Add**.
  4. *Added*: one line saying where it now is in the tree, with **Show documents** (or **Open Changes waiting** for code) and **Add something else**.
  Asking Ordin in words for a code repository opens the same screen.
- **GitLab is a place like GitHub (ADR-051).** A workspace links Markdown documents or a code repository on the company's GitLab. The person signs in with their own GitLab account the first time. Words are the same as for GitHub: "saved to the shared copy for everyone working on it"; for people working with code, a GitLab change is proposed as a **merge request** where GitHub says pull request.
- **Jira issues** (*added in revision 0.54.0, ADR-106; UX-A75*). On **Add to this workspace**, **Jira issue** has a box for the issue's address ("Paste the issue's address, or search") and a search by words over the issues the person can see; each result shows its key, summary and status, with **Add**. A linked issue sits in the tree under **Jira** as "ABC-12 · [summary]", holding **Summary and description**, one row per comment ("Comment by Sam Lee, 21 Oct"), one row per attachment by its file name, and **Sub-tasks and linked issues**, a list that names each issue with **Open in Jira** and **Add to this workspace**. Everything is read-only: the actions are **Ask Ordin about this** and **Open in Jira**. When the issue changes in Jira, the changed rows carry the changed mark (ADR-079), "Changed in Jira, 10:40", and **Bring in the new version**; nothing is replaced until then. **Remove from this workspace** uses the confirmation of UX-A57 and says "Nothing in Jira changes." ordin's own handover comments are not listed as comments; they are shown as the handover (§5.28).
- **Connecting Jira** (*added in revision 0.54.0, ADR-106; UX-A75*). The first time a Jira issue or a Jira handover place is used, **Connect Jira** opens beside the step that needs it, and later stays in **Settings › Jira**. It asks for the site address ("acme.atlassian.net") and the email address of the person's Atlassian account, then explains in three numbered lines how to make an API token, with **Open Atlassian's token page** (the browser opens) and a box to paste the token: "The token is kept in this computer's keychain. ordin uses it only to read the issues you link and to hand work over after you approve." When the organisation profile names the company's Atlassian app, **Sign in with Atlassian** is offered first, with the token as **Use a token instead**. When the Atlassian organisation blocks tokens and there is no company app, Settings says: "Your company's Atlassian settings don't allow tokens. Ask IT to turn on Jira for ordin." with **Send to IT** (R-103). The server's chat has no Jira.
- **Signing in is asked when first needed**, with the reason: "Sign in to SharePoint so Ordin can read the supplier folder." The person signs in with their own account; the company's sites come from its settings.
- **A workspace shows its contents in one place**: everything linked, grouped by where it lives; the agents and workflows allowed in it; its conversation and history.
- **Access is per workspace.** "Release planner can read and change Supplier quotes in this workspace." Giving access in one workspace gives nothing in another, and the screen says so the first time.
- **Everything starts in a workspace.** Tasks, crews and conversations belong to the workspace they started in. *Needs you* shows which workspace each item belongs to.
- **Removing a workspace never deletes documents, and goes to the Bin (UX-A68).** It is asked of Ordin, chosen under the open workspace, or chosen from any workspace's row in the switcher (§4, UX-A13). It changes only this computer, so it uses a confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*, UX-A57): "Move this workspace to the Bin?", with "It goes to the Bin, with its links, conversation and history. You can restore it from there. Your documents stay where they are.", **Move to Bin** and **Cancel**. When unsent changes exist, the confirmation names how many and in which documents, and says they go with it. Ordin's line afterwards offers **Undo**.
- **The Bin (UX-A68).** ordin's Bin is drawn and named as the computer's own: **Bin** on macOS, **Recycle Bin** on Windows. It sits at the foot of the workspace tree, with its count, and opens as a screen in the centre, like Home. Each removed workspace keeps its links, conversation, history and changes not yet sent back, and shows **Restore** (which brings it back as it was) and **Delete now…**; the Bin has **Empty Bin…**. Both deletions name what is lost and ask once, with "This can't be undone." A workspace stays in the Bin until the person deletes it or empties the Bin; nothing is removed by age. *Added in revision 0.44.0 by the user's decision of January 2026; this replaces deleting a workspace at once, which P6 did not allow.*

### 5.14 Confluence

Source: G15, ADR-026, ADR-029.

- **Confluence sits in the workspace's tree** with the other origins, by space and page names. Pages open as readable previews with their attachments. **Ask Ordin about this** and **Open in Confluence** are the actions.
- **Changes Ordin makes to a Confluence page** are sent back like any other origin: listed in the request's approval card, with the page's before-and-after view (§5.6).
- **A page made from a Markdown original** is a published copy (§5.6, ADR-069): it follows the original when kept up to date, and reads "Published copy of …" at the top.
- **Publishing a folder from this computer to Confluence** follows ADR-026 for the person's own documents: linking is one approval ("Publish Supplier quotes to Confluence?" with Where, Affects and Changes, and the buttons **Review files · Link and publish · Not now** (*amended in revision 0.39.1, UI-D78: **Not now**, as §5.5 allows*)). After linking, the person's own edits are published automatically; **Ordin's changes in a linked folder are never published automatically** and always go through the request's approval card.
- **The tree and list show publishing state** with a label and an icon, never colour alone: "Published", "Updating…", "Ready to publish", "Changed in Confluence", "Not published", "Changed by Ordin — not yet sent back".
- **Ready to publish** collects new, renamed and removed files in a linked folder, with a count in *Needs you*; **Publish checked items…** opens an approval card for them (§5.5); nothing is published from the list itself.
- **Changed in Confluence** shows who changed the page and when, with **Bring a copy back** and **Keep mine**. Until the person chooses, that file is not pushed.
- **What becomes what is said plainly before publishing**: "Text documents become pages. Other files are attached to the folder's page as they are."
- **Sign-in is asked when first needed**: "Sign in to Confluence to see the pages linked to this workspace."

Source: ADR-028.

Ordin shows things by choosing from a fixed catalogue. The application draws each one with its own code from data Ordin supplies; Ordin never supplies layout, styling or code. The person never sees the word "block"; each is named by what it holds.

| Kind | Used for | Typical actions |
|---|---|---|
| Message | Ordin's reply, with attached results | — |
| Question with choices | One question, two to four choices, an "other" answer in words | The choices |
| Form | A task's inputs (§5.3), or typed fields Ordin composes | The task's verb |

Rules:

- **Approval cards are not in the catalogue.** The application builds them from the action itself (§5.5). Nothing Ordin shows may contain a button that approves, sends, posts or publishes; those buttons exist only on approval cards. Confirmations (§5.5 *Confirmations*) are not in the catalogue either.
- **Fixed screens outside the catalogue.** These screens are the application's own, drawn the same way every time from the application's data; Ordin can open them, but supplies none of their content or layout: **Add to this workspace** (§5.13), with its lists of places, Email links and the Telegram chats the bot has joined (§5.22, §5.25); the **document page** of one document (§5.6, UX-A52); the **workflow** screen and its editor (§5.20); the **agent draft** (§5.26); **Add an agent repository…** (§5.2); *from revision 0.37.0* **Parts of [agent repository]**, the **part draft** and **Withdraw** (§5.27, ADR-091); *from revision 0.38.0* the **handover draft**, the **handover screen** and **Lifecycle: [name]** (§5.28, ADR-092); *from revision 0.39.0* **What [agent] is made of**, the **part page** and **Show the installed files** (§5.29, ADR-093), and the **import** screen (§5.30, ADR-094); **Earlier conversations** and an earlier conversation opened read-only (§5.12); Home, Search or go to…, and Settings (§4, §5.17). The chat on an ordin server (§5.23) is not a screen of ordin at all: it is the chat app's own, and the prototype draws a phone only to show it.
- **Every action in a shown item does what its label says** and goes through the same rules as a typed request. A form's verb starts a task; it does not approve anything that leaves the computer.
- **Where each kind appears (UX-A15, UX-A35).** Question with choices appears in the conversation in full. Form, crew plan and approval card open as screens at once, with a **Waiting for you** card in the conversation and an entry in *Needs you* until they are done (UX-A53). An agent proposal opens as the agent library screen (UX-A33). Documents, preview, changes, plan and progress, result, reports (and a single table, chart or key figures) and what Ordin remembers appear on a screen, with a one-line card in the conversation that names it and carries **Open**. A result that offers something and waits for an answer ("Save as workflow?") stays in the conversation. A workflow opens as a screen (§5.20). An open screen stays current: a crew's progress keeps moving, and a document list reflects changes.
- **Close.** Every item in the conversation carries **Close** once it no longer waits for the person, folding it to one line with **Show again** (§4, UX-A12). A screen is closed with its tab's close control; it can be opened again from its card or from the tree.
- **When something cannot be shown,** because the catalogue has no fitting kind or the data failed the application's checks, Ordin says it in a message instead. The person never sees a broken or empty item.
- **Shown items look the same every time.** The same kind of thing is always drawn the same way, so the person learns it once.
- **The engineering views are not in the catalogue.** The code editor, *Changes waiting* and the terminal are fixed views of engineering mode (§5.11); they open as screens and follow the same tab rules (UX-A15). Ordin's message about them carries a one-line card with **Open**, like any screen.
- Shown items follow §3's words, §8's state labels and §10's accessibility baseline, like any screen.

### 5.16 Ordin as go-between

Source: P9; ADR-027.

- **Agents' first questions go to Ordin.** When a member of a crew, or a single agent, asks for clarification, Ordin answers from the request, the workspace's documents and what it remembers. The person sees "Ordin answered 2 questions from Supplier analyst" with the answers one click away.
- **What reaches the person is a decision only they can make**: a choice of direction, a missing fact, an approval, a risk. Each is one or two sentences, with the options and Ordin's recommendation: "Two quotes use different currencies. I suggest converting both to euros at today's rate — or should I use the rate in the contract?"
- **Problems are translated.** Errors and refusals from agents reach the person as one plain sentence on what happened and what it means for their request, with one action (P7). The agent's own wording is in the details, not in front.
- **Results are summarised.** When work finishes, Ordin says in a few sentences what was done, what changed and what needs the person, before any detail.
- **Ordin says when it guessed.** An answer Ordin gave an agent on the person's behalf is listed in the result, so the person can correct it.

### 5.17 Keeping track across workspaces

Source: G25, ADR-058 to ADR-060, ADR-011, ADR-025.

People who work in many workspaces need to see what happened across them without opening each one. Home, the tree's changed marks and the search box are drawn by the application's own code from what is on the computer, with no message from Ordin, so they cost nothing on the person's plan.

- **Home (UX-A29).** **Home**, at the top of the side rail under the brand, carries a count of new items and opens Home as a screen. It has three sections:
  - **Waiting for you**: the same items as *Needs you*, grouped by workspace. *Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).* Each row is itself the action and opens on a click or Enter; only the oldest item waiting keeps a primary **Open**, the rest show a chevron, so one thing stands out as next. *From revision 0.38.0 (ADR-092) it also holds "Handed to you", "Returned to you" and "No answer from [name] in [N] days" (§5.28).* *Needs you* is the first tab of the conversation panel (UX-A37).
  - **Running now**: every task and crew run in every workspace, with its state label (§8) and **Show progress**.
  - **Since you last looked**: finished tasks and crew runs, documents someone else changed where they live, knowledge pages that may be out of date, and replies from agents that answer in Slack, newest first. Each row names its workspace and its time, with **Open** and, for finished work, **Run again…**.
  - **Filter.** **All workspaces**, or one workspace. Items new since the person last opened Home are marked **new**, with a label as well as an icon.
  - **Rows open where things are.** A row opens the item in its own workspace, switching to it first; an approval is decided on its own screen in that workspace, never on Home.
  - **Empty.** With nothing to show, each section says so in one line ("Nothing running now").
- **Nothing on return (UX-A52, ADR-079).** Opening a workspace writes nothing into its conversation. Documents changed elsewhere are marked in the tree (§5.6); finished work, knowledge pages that may be out of date and new messages are on Home and as counts on their tree groups. Asking "what's new?" or "what did I miss?" is answered in words, with **Show them** for the changed documents and **Everything on Home**. *Until revision 0.23.0 a note, Since you were last here, listed them at the end of the conversation.*
- **Search or go to… (UX-A30).** Below **Home** in the side rail, and with Ctrl+K (⌘K on a Mac), the box opens as a dialog over the window, with the cursor in it.
  - **Results**, as the person types, in groups: **Go to** (workspaces, agents, workflows, dashboards with "Dashboard · as of [time]", knowledge pages, earlier conversations, documents by name); **Found in pages and documents** (matching text in knowledge pages and in each workspace's documents); **Actions** (Home, New workspace, Add documents, Summarise, Clear, Earlier conversations, Settings — the same names as the conversation's own buttons, §5.12). The number of results is announced as it changes.
  - **Other workspaces.** A result from another workspace names that workspace ("· Q4 forecast"); choosing it switches to that workspace first.
  - **For the person only.** Agents keep searching only the workspace they work in. To have an agent use something from another workspace, the person adds it to this one (§5.13); the dialog says so on a result from another workspace: "To use this here, add it to this workspace."
  - **It decides nothing.** An action opens the same screen, form or card as asking Ordin. Nothing is sent and nothing leaves the computer.
  - **Keys.** The arrow keys move, Enter opens, Escape closes and returns focus to where it was. The dialog's footer names them: "↑↓ to move · Enter to open · Esc to close".
  - **Words.** "search everywhere for Kestrel" opens it with "Kestrel" filled in.
- **Run again… (UX-A31).** On a finished task's or crew run's progress screen and on its Home row. It opens the same task form (§5.3) or crew plan (§5.7), filled with the previous inputs; a newer version of an input is used and named ("Quote — Kestrel.pdf: newer version since last time"). Nothing starts until the person presses the form's button or **Start**. Asking "run that again" does the same.
- **Every week… goes to IT (UX-A31).** Beside **Run again…** on a finished run's progress screen is **Every week…**. Choosing it, or asking to repeat work on a schedule, has Ordin say in one line: "This computer may be asleep or closed, so repeating work is set up on the company's agent service." It offers **Ask IT to set it up…**, which opens an approval card (§5.5) naming the agent, the task, the inputs by name only and how often. Nothing is scheduled on the computer (ADR-011). Once sent, the prototype has Ordin say "Sent to IT. Their answer will appear in Needs you. Nothing is scheduled on this computer." Whether IT's answer can reach *Needs you* depends on OQ-53 (10 — Risks), so the second sentence is not settled (§6). *Amended in revision 0.48.0 (OQ-53, settled by the user on January 2026, option b): IT answers by its usual route and nothing appears in* Needs you*. Once sent, Ordin says "Sent to IT. IT will reply to you directly. Nothing is scheduled on this computer."* *Amended in revision 0.53.0 (ADR-105): Ordin's line says ordin keeps no timetable and the company's scheduler can start the work; where the person has an ordin server that reads NATS events, the card asks IT for a scheduled event, and the person turns on the matching trigger once IT names the subject.*

### 5.18 Using your plan

Source: G26, ADR-062, ADR-049, ADR-044.

People pay for agents with their own plan, and Ordin's coordination adds turns of its own. Most of the saving is built in and needs no choice (ADR-062); Settings offers one choice, with details for those who want them.

- **Settings › Using your plan (UX-A32).**
  - **How Ordin uses your plan:** three options, each with one line: *Save my plan* ("Smaller models wherever they do the job; asks before medium or heavy work"), *Balanced* ("Ordin picks the model for each step; the most capable only where it matters") and *Best results* ("The most capable models for talking, planning and writing"). *Balanced* is the default.
  - Then, each with one line saying what it does: **Answer simple requests without the model**, **Reuse an answer when nothing has changed**, **Give agents only the parts they need**, **Summarise long conversations**, **After a break of more than an hour, continue from a summary**, **Ask before work estimated as**, **Agents working at the same time**.
  - **More choices**, folded: the model level for each kind of work (changing one shows the choice above as *Custom*), **Show usage on each run** and the daily pause.
  - **Today**, at the top of the section: "Today you've used about a fifth of a typical day's plan. Most work ran on the lighter, cheaper setting.", with "N model turns · N% on smaller models · N answered by ordin without the model" under **Details** (Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).; it said the model turns first). The daily pause and per-run usage are said as shares of a typical day's use, never in model turns. No remaining allowance is shown, because ordin cannot see it.
  - A setting the company fixes is shown, disabled, with "Set by your company".
- **Estimated use (UX-A32).** A crew plan (§5.7) and a large task show **Estimated use: Light**, **Medium** or **Heavy**, with "about a tenth of a typical day's use of your plan" and "Based on …"; the model turns are under **Details** (Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).). When the estimate reaches the person's level, a task without a plan asks in one line first, with **Start** and **Not now**.
- **Reused answers.** An answer shown again says so: "Answered earlier today from the same versions", with **Ask again**.
- **After a break.** "Continuing from a summary of this conversation", with **Use the whole conversation**.
- **Results.** A result produced on a smaller model offers **Try again with a more capable model**.
- **Words.** "save my plan", "use less of my plan" and "use the best model" set the choice, as the setting does, and say so in one line.

### 5.19 How Ordin talks

Source: G27, ADR-063, P7, P9, ADR-042.

- **Always plain and brief.** Ordin gives the answer or the result first, then at most one recommendation and one next step, in everyday words and short sentences, one idea to a sentence. It asks one question at a time. It never uses the words in §3's column *Never shown outside Diagnostics* outside workspaces with code. Facts, numbers and warnings are never softened or dropped for tone or length.
- **Settings › How Ordin talks to you (UX-A34).** The section starts with one line on what is fixed: "Ordin always uses plain words and keeps it short. Choose how it sounds." Then:
  - **Tone:** *Friendly* (default), *Neutral*, *Warm*, *Direct*; **Formality:** *Everyday* (default), *Formal*; **Length:** *Brief* (default), *Shortest*.
  - **Call me**, empty by default, used in greetings only.
  - **Notes for Ordin**, up to 300 characters, with the example "I'm new to procurement; explain a term the first time you use it" and the line "Notes change how Ordin explains, never what it may do".
  - **Preview**: "How Ordin would say it", one example message that changes as the person chooses, drawn by the application.
  - A default the company set is shown as "Your company's default".
- **Length in the conversation.** A message longer than the chosen length shows its first part and **Show more**. "Tell me more" gives the full answer once.
- **Words.** "be more formal", "keep it shorter", "be warmer", "call me Sam" and, from revision 0.58.0, "speak French" change the setting; Ordin replies in one line in the new style ("Done — I'll keep it shorter from now on").
- **What tone never touches.** Approval cards, errors, run states and who-acts statements keep their fixed wording (§5.5, §6, §8).

### 5.20 Workflows

Source: ADR-064, ADR-027, ADR-046, ADR-060, §5.7.

- **A workflow is a screen in the centre.** It opens from the tree's **Workflows** group, from **Search or go to…**, from a crew plan's **Open the workflow**, or by asking ("show me the supplier rates refresh"). Opening it adds nothing to the conversation (§12 item 20). The screen shows, top to bottom:
  - the name, the purpose in one line, and where it came from ("Saved from the run of 16 Oct", "Duplicated from Supplier rates refresh, version 3", "Imported from a file on 17 Oct", "Drafted by Ordin from your request");
  - **Asks for**: the inputs it needs at the start, each with its label and whether it is required;
  - **Agents**: each agent with its role in one line;
  - **Steps**, numbered. Each step: the agent, what it does in one line, and what it uses ("uses: Supplier rates Q3.xlsx", "uses: step 1's result"). Steps that run at the same time share a number with a letter (2a, 2b) and the note "at the same time". The marks **Asks you first** and **Ordin checks the result** show on the step, in words, with an icon that never carries the meaning alone;
  - **Limits**, as plain sentences (§5.7);
  - **Versions**: each with its date and one line of what changed, and **Restore** on earlier ones;
  - **Last runs**, at most five, each with its date, its state (§8) and **Open**.
- **Actions.** **Run…** (opens its crew plan, §5.7; unavailable, with the reason, while an agent it uses is missing), **Change**, **Duplicate…**, **Export…**, **Share…** (only while the workflow is not shared, *Shared workflows* below), **Rename…** and **Remove…**, at the foot of the screen. The same actions are in the workflow's tree node menu, where its main action is **Run…**.
- **Changing.** **Change** turns the same screen into its editor, never a separate builder:
  - each step's line becomes a text field; each step has **Move up**, **Move down**, **Remove step**, the two marks as switches, and **Run with the step before** or **Run on its own**;
  - limits have the − and + of §5.7, never past the company's maximum; inputs can be renamed, made required or optional, or removed;
  - **Ask Ordin to add a step…** puts the question in the message box; Ordin proposes the step on the screen, marked **Proposed by Ordin**, with **Keep** and **Leave out**;
  - **Save** checks the workflow and shows any problem on the step it concerns, in one sentence with one action ("Supplier analyst isn't in this workspace — Find in the library"); a saved change makes a new version, and the screen says "Saved as version 4". **Cancel** discards the edit. If anything changed, it first asks on the screen itself, in place of **Save** and **Cancel**: "Discard your changes?" with **Discard changes** and **Keep changing**. This is not a dialog, because the edit is still in front of the person.
  - Asking in words ("check prices before the summary") opens the editor with Ordin's change proposed, and nothing is saved until the person presses **Save**.
- **Duplicating.** **Duplicate…** asks for the name ("Copy of Supplier rates refresh") and the workspace, this one first. The copy opens on its screen. In another workspace, an agent missing there is listed at the top with **Find in the library**, and **Run…** waits until it is present.
- **Renaming.** **Rename…** opens a small panel on the screen with one field, "New name for the workflow", at most 80 characters, and **Rename** and **Cancel**. An empty name is refused in place with "Give the workflow a name."; a name another workflow in the workspace already has is refused with "There's already a workflow called “[name]” here. Choose another name." A rename changes only the name: the versions, the last runs and, for a shared workflow, where it is shared stay as they were, and the screen notes "Renamed from “[old name]”."
- **Exporting.** **Export…** shows one line before the system's save dialog: "The file holds the steps, the agents' names and the limits. It holds no documents, links, folder access or history." The file is named "[name].workflow.yaml". In a workspace that links a code repository, the panel also has **What's in the file**, which shows the file's text before it is saved; other workspaces never show it (§3, G10). *Added in revision 0.36.2 by the user's decision of January 2026 (UI-D81).*
- **Importing.** **Import workflow…** on the Workflows group and in the workspace's actions menu (⋯) opens the system's file picker; dropping a workflow file on the tree or the conversation does the same (§5.6).
  - The imported workflow opens on its screen marked **Imported — not saved yet**, every step shown in full, with **Save to this workspace** and **Discard**. It is never run before it is saved.
  - What the check found is listed at the top, one line each: an agent not in the workspace, with **Find in the library**; a limit lowered to the company's maximum ("The file asked for 40 rounds; your company's maximum is 30"); a name already used, with " (2)" added.
  - A file this release cannot read says so in one sentence ("This workflow was made by a newer ordin. Update ordin to import it."), and nothing is added.
  - The screen says once: "A workflow file can't give any agent access to your documents, and every send back still needs your approval."
- **Removing.** **Remove…** opens a confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*): what goes (the workflow and its versions on this computer), what stays (its agents, its past runs in history, and any copy already shared to a repository).
- **Control blocks (ADR-065, UX-A39).** Steps and blocks form one numbered outline; steps inside a block are numbered under it (2.1, 2.1.1), across its paths.
  - Each block reads as a sentence with its limit: "**For each** item in “Contracts” — at most 20 items, 3 at a time"; "**If** any notice period is longer than 60 days — decided by Ordin, from these words"; "**Ask me** Send the review deck now?" with its choices as path names; "**Repeat until** “You approve the note” — at most 3 times"; "**Run the workflow** “Supplier rates refresh”".
  - An If's paths sit side by side on wide screens and one under the other on narrow ones, each with its name; an empty path reads "Carry on with the next step".
  - An agent step's **If it fails** shows only when it is not the default: "If it fails: skip it and carry on" or "If it fails: ask me".
  - While changing: **Add…** at the end of every list and path offers *An agent step — ask Ordin*, *If… / otherwise*, *Ask me…*, *For each…*, *Repeat until…*, *Run another workflow…*; a block's fields (who decides, the words, the list, the maximum with − and +, the workflow, the path names, **Add a choice**) are changed in place; **Remove block** says it removes the steps inside.
  - The check's problems show on the block: "Say what decides the path.", "Add a step to at least one path.", "It uses step 2.1's result, which exists only when “Yes” is taken at step 2.", "This would run “Contract review” again inside itself."
- **Show as diagram.** **Show as diagram**, above the steps, draws the same workflow as a flowchart, for reading only, with the line "For reading. The numbered list is the same workflow; change it there." **Show as a list** returns. The diagram has a text name and never replaces the list for a screen reader. *From revision 0.12.1 (UX-A40)* each kind has its own colour **and** its own shape: agent step, a blue box; *Asks you first*, an amber box with a corner flag; **If**, a violet diamond; **Ask me**, an orange hexagon; **For each**, a teal dashed frame; **Repeat until**, a green rounded frame; **Run another workflow**, an indigo double box; path names in grey pills. A key under the diagram names each shape. Colours have dark-theme values and keep text contrast at 4.5:1 or more (§10).
- **Running with blocks.** The progress screen lists each path taken with why and who decided ("2. Took “Yes”: Kestrel's notice period is 120 days — one Ordin turn"), each item a **For each** reached, and each time round a **Repeat until** ("Not yet — time 2 of at most 3"). A person-decided **Repeat until** asks "Has this happened: “You approve the note”?" with **Yes** and **Not yet**; at its maximum it asks whether to carry on or stop.
- **Shared workflows (ADR-066).**
  - **Workflows in linked repositories…**, on the Workflows group, opens a screen listing the workflow files in the workspace's linked GitHub and GitLab folders and in **Company workflows**, each with where it lives and who changed it, and **Add…** or **Open**. Asking ("show me the shared workflows") does the same. **Add…** checks the file as an import does.
  - A shared workflow's screen starts with "Shared in GitHub — acme/supplier-portal › workflows, version from 12 Oct by Priya Shah", with the origin's icon. Its tree node's name for screen readers says "shared in GitHub".
  - A newer version: "A newer version is in GitHub, changed today by Sam Lee: [one line]" with **See what changed** (the newer outline above this one) and **Use the newer version**. A crew plan of that workflow says a newer version exists and that this run uses the version shown.
  - Changed here: "Changed here — not yet sent back" with **Send back…** and **Keep it as my own**. When both changed, both outlines are shown and the person chooses.
  - **Share…** on a workflow not yet shared chooses the repository folder, then opens the approval card as a screen: "Share “Supplier rates refresh” in GitHub?" — where, the file, and "One file is committed and pushed, or a pull request is opened if the folder needs a review… Everyone who can open the repository can use it."
- **Accessible.** Steps and blocks are a nested numbered list; marks, moves, joins, block fields and **Add…** are reachable and announced by keyboard and screen reader (§10). The diagram is for reading only; there is no canvas to edit (UX-A38, UX-A39).

### 5.21 Reports and dashboards

Source: ADR-067.

- **What a report is.** A screen with a title, "as of" a date and time, and up to twelve parts in order — tables, charts and key figures — each with its own heading. An agent sends one as the result of its step; Ordin may show one too. The conversation gets a one-line card ("Report: Q3 supplier rates — 3 parts") with **Open**; the report opens as a tab, at once only when it answers what the person just asked (§5.15).
- **Every number says where it comes from.** Under each part, **Where these numbers come from** names the documents with the version read, the company system and when it was read, or the agent that worked them out and from what ("Worked out by Supplier analyst from Q3 rates.xlsx"). A document named there opens on **Open**.
- **Tables** sort by any column and filter with a search box; numbers align right, money shows its currency, dates follow the person's locale (§10, ADR-042). Long tables load in pages and stay usable at 10,000 rows.
- **Charts** are bar, line, area, scatter or pie. Each has a text title and axis titles, a legend that also names each series in words, and **Show as table**, which shows the same rows. Series colours come from the application's chart palette, in light and dark themes, never from the agent; a chart never relies on colour alone, so lines also differ by marker and bars by order and label (§10).
- **Key figures** show a label, a big value with its unit, and a change only when an earlier value is named ("up 8% on Q2").
- **Keep as dashboard…** asks for a name and saves the report in the workspace's **Dashboards** group, with the task or workflow and inputs that made it. A name already used by a dashboard in the workspace is refused in place: "There's already a dashboard called “[name]”. Choose another name." A dashboard says "as of" when it was made. **Refresh** runs the same work again with the same inputs (§5.4 *Run again*), naming any newer input first; it runs only when the person presses it. **Every week…** asks IT, as for any repeated work (UX-A31).
- **After a refresh** the dashboard says what changed in one line at its top: "Refreshed — as of 17 Oct, 15:20. Spend so far went from £412k to £418k; three more invoices came in, from Acme and Kestrel." Home's *Since you last looked* gets a line, "Refreshed [name]".
- **Removing a dashboard.** **Remove [name]…**, in the dashboard's actions in the tree, uses a confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*, UX-A57): "Remove the dashboard “[name]”?", with "The documents and systems its numbers came from are not touched. Files saved from it stay.", **Remove** and **Cancel**.
- **Save as…** offers *Spreadsheet*, *PDF*, *Slides* and *Document*, writing a new file in the workspace. Sending it anywhere goes through an approval card.
- **What cannot be shown.** A part that fails the application's check is left out and the report says so in one line, with the reason ("One part could not be shown, so it was left out. Its numbers didn't say where they came from.") and **Ask Ordin why**. Ordin explains without being asked, and asks the agent to send the part again with its source: "One chart Spend explorer sent didn't say where its numbers came from, so I left it out; I've asked it to send it again with its source." *Amended in revision 0.36.0: this said Ordin explains only if asked.* An agent that would show its own screens is named in the start-up report: "Shows its own screens, which ordin does not display" (P5).
- **Nothing in a report acts.** A report has no buttons of its own beyond those above, **Ask Ordin about this** and, on a part left out, **Ask Ordin why**; it cannot approve, send or change anything (§5.15).

### 5.22 Email

Source: ADR-070, ADR-071, R-57, R-58, R-59, R-60, UX-A44, UX-A45.

Email is part of a workspace like its documents: the person links the email the work needs, Ordin and the agents the person allows read it, and nothing is sent until the person has seen the exact message and chosen **Send**.

- **Linking email.** **Add to this workspace** offers **Email** beside SharePoint, Confluence and the others. The person chooses a mail folder ("Inbox › Suppliers") or people and companies ("everyone at Kestrel and Acme"); Ordin can propose one from the request ("Link email from Kestrel and Acme to this workspace?"). The first time, the screen asks the person to sign in once (see *Signing in to the company's mail server*; with Microsoft 365, "Sign in with Microsoft to reach your email"). The link reads: "Ordin and the agents you allow can read these messages. Nothing is sent without you."
- **Signing in to the company's mail server (UX-A45, ADR-071).** The screen is titled "Sign in to your company's email" and shows:
  - the server as a fixed line, not a field: "Your company's mail server: mail.example.com (set by your company)";
  - with single sign-on, one button: **Sign in with your company account**;
  - otherwise, above the password, *Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).* "Your company's mail server doesn't use a browser sign-in, so this is the one password ordin asks for. It goes only to [server] and is kept in this computer's keychain."; then **Email address** (filled with the person's work address, with "Filled in from your company account. Change it only if you use another address for email.") and **App password** or **Password**, as the profile says, with **Show**; under **Password**, the line "If your company gives you an app password for email, use that." and the note "ordin keeps this in this computer's keychain and uses it only to reach your company's mail server.";
  - **Sign in**. A refused password reads "Your mail server didn't accept this password. Check it and try again." ordin makes no second attempt until the person presses **Sign in** again;
  - a server that can't be reached securely: "Your company's mail server can't be reached securely. Ask IT." with **Ask IT**. There is no way to connect without encryption.
- **Email in the tree.** The workspace's **Email** group lists each link with its count of new messages. Opening a link shows an Email list as a screen: newest first, with sender, subject, date and attachments. A sender outside the company carries **Outside the company** with an icon, never colour alone. Opening a message shows it as an Email screen with **Reply with Ordin**, **Save attachment** and **Ask Ordin about this**.
- **Asking.** "Has Kestrel replied?" is answered by Ordin from the linked email, citing the message ("Yes — Dan Moss, 14 Oct, 'Q3 quote', with one attachment") with **Open**.
- **Letting an agent read or draft.** The first time an agent needs email in a workspace, Ordin asks in one line: "Let Supplier analyst read and draft email for Kestrel and Acme in this workspace?" with **Allow read and draft**, **Read only** and **Not now**. The agent's page in the workspace shows the grant and lets the person change it.
- **Drafts.** An agent's email arrives as a draft on its own screen, with a one-line card in the conversation: "Draft reply to Dan Moss (Kestrel) · drafted by Supplier analyst" with **Open**. The draft shows To, Cc, subject, the message and attachments, and the buttons **Send…**, **Edit**, **Save to mail drafts** and **Discard**, in that order. **Edit** uses the same toolbar as documents (§5.6); the editor fills the central pane, with To and Subject kept above the message (UX-A59). **Save to mail drafts** ("Save to Outlook drafts" or "Save to Gmail drafts" when the company uses those) saves a copy in the mailbox's Drafts folder; the draft still sends only from ordin's approval card or from the person's own mail app. *Amended in revision 0.36.0: **Save to mail drafts** was in the draft's ⋯ menu; the prototype shows it as a visible button between **Edit** and **Discard**.* **Discard** uses a confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*): "Discard this draft?", with "The draft is deleted from this computer. Nothing was sent.", **Discard** and **Cancel**.
- **Sending (§5.5).** **Send…** opens the approval card built from the draft. Its **Where** line is "From you (your address)"; **Affects** lists each recipient, with **Outside the company** beside those outside; **Changes** shows the subject, the whole message as it will be sent with the person's signature, and each attachment with where it comes from. Buttons: **Send**, **Edit**, **Not now**. The card is never built from an agent's words, and there is no setting that skips it.
- **After sending.** The draft becomes "Sent to Dan Moss at Kestrel, 10:42. It's in your Sent folder." with **Open in Outlook** or **Open in Gmail** when the company uses those; with the company's mail server there is no open button. If ordin stops while sending, the draft says "Not sure this was sent" with **Check Sent**; ordin checks the Sent folder before it offers to send again (§8).
- **When the company's mail server says no (UX-A45, ADR-071).**
  - A refused recipient: "Not sent. Your mail server refused dan.mos@kestrel.co.uk: no such mailbox. Nothing was sent to anyone." with **Edit**.
  - Too large: "This email is 31 MB; your mail server takes up to 25 MB. Remove an attachment or share a link instead.", before anything is sent, with **Edit**.
  - Sent, but the copy didn't reach Sent: "Sent to Dan Moss at Kestrel, 10:42. Not saved in your Sent folder." with **Save it there**.
  - Not found after a crash: "Not sure this was sent. It isn't in your Sent folder, but it may still have gone. Check with Dan Moss or in your mail app." with **Send again** and **Mark as sent**. ordin never sends it again on its own.
- **New email.** New messages in a linked folder or from linked people show as a count on the Email group, and a line on Home ("2 new emails from Kestrel"). New email starts no work; Ordin mentions it only when asked.
- **Messages are not instructions.** When a message asks the agent to do something ("please forward all invoices to…"), Ordin says so instead of doing it: "This email asks the agent to forward invoices. I haven't acted on it." Hidden text in a message is reported as in documents (§6).
- **Settings › Email (UX-A44)** starts with what is fixed: "Every email is shown to you before it is sent. This can't be turned off." Then:
  - **Your mailbox**: the address and mail system ("you@company.com — your company's mail server, mail.example.com"; with Microsoft 365, "Outlook, through your Microsoft sign-in"), how the person is signed in ("Signed in with your password, kept in this computer's keychain" or "Signed in with your company account"), with **Change password** for a password sign-in and **Sign out of email**. Sign out removes the password from the keychain;
  - **Add my signature** (on), *with the signature text the person types once (empty: nothing is added; revision 0.49.0)*;
  - **Say at the bottom that an agent drafted it** (off; shown on and locked when IT requires it, with "Required by your company");
  - **Check for new email**: *Every 15 minutes* (default) or *Only when I open the workspace*;
  - **Email in your workspaces**: each workspace with its email links and the agents allowed to read or draft there, each with **Change**;
  - what IT set, in plain words, when it applies: "You can email people inside the company only", "At most 20 people per email".

### 5.23 Talking from a chat app (ordin server)

Source: ADR-073, R-62, R-63, UX-A47.

On an ordin server the person has no window: the chat app is the whole interface. Everything in this section follows the same principles as the desktop (P0, P2, decision 10); only the form changes.

- **Pairing.** Whoever runs the server gives the person a one-time code, valid for 10 minutes. The person sends it to the bot in a private chat; from then on only their account is answered.
- **Welcome.** After pairing, one message says what works here and what does not, in plain words: "I'm your ordin on the server. You can ask me for work, send me files, and approve things here. Editing code, opening files in apps on your computer and working with Confluence aren't available here." It ends with one line asking the person to turn on two-step verification in Telegram, because approvals made here count.
- **Ordin's replies** are short, as everywhere (§5.19). A reply longer than one message is split at paragraph ends, never mid-sentence. A voice note is answered "I can't listen to voice notes here. Please type your message." (ADR-078).
- **Approval cards** carry the same sentence as on the desktop, and three buttons: **Approve**, **Not now** and **Show details**. **Show details** sends the full content (the whole email, the full list of changes) as a separate message or file. After a decision, the card is edited to say what happened ("Approved by you, 10:42 · Sent to Priya Shah") and its buttons go. Typing "yes" never approves; Ordin answers "Use the buttons on the card to approve."
- **Questions with fixed choices** come as buttons; open questions as a plain message the person answers in words. A task form is asked one field at a time, then shown as one summary with the task's own verb as the button (§5.3).
- **Blocks as text.** A table becomes up to 10 lines with "and 14 more" and the full table attached as a file; a chart becomes its key figures; progress becomes the Now sentence and "step N of M" (§5.4), sent when a step changes, not more often than once a minute for one run.
- **Files.** A file the person sends goes into the workspace's inbox, and Ordin says where. A file larger than Telegram allows is saved in the workspace and named in the reply ("Saved as Supplier summary.xlsx in Procurement › Results; it's too large to send here").
- **What needs the person** arrives as it happens: approvals, crew questions, finished work and failures, nothing else (UX-A4).
- **Stop and status.** **/stop** pauses every run and says how many; **/status** lists what is running in one line each. Both are listed in the welcome.
- **Secrets never go in the chat.** When a sign-in needs a password, Ordin says it must be set up on the server by whoever runs it, and sends nothing to type. A device sign-in arrives as the provider's link and code, with the line "Enter this code on Microsoft's page, not here."
- **Messages from anyone else** get no answer at all, so the bot reveals nothing about its owner.

- **Two channels (UX-A50, ADR-076).** With Telegram and Slack both paired, Ordin answers where the person wrote, and tells the other channel once: "Ordin is working on your Telegram request. Cards also appear here." A card appears in both; once decided in one, the other reads "Decided in Slack, 10:44" with no buttons. After a restart, one message on the last channel used: "ordin restarted at 03:12; 2 runs resumed, 1 approval still waiting.", with **Show it**, which sends the waiting card again. In Slack the commands are words in the direct message: **stop**, **status**, **run**, **triggers**.
- **A channel that is down.** When one channel has not been reachable for 10 minutes, Ordin says so once on the other: "Slack hasn't been reachable for 10 minutes. I'm still here; Slack cards wait until it's back."
- **A sign-in that has ended.** When the server's sign-in to the agent service ends, Ordin says so at once, on the last channel used, with the step to take and who takes it: "Your Claude Code sign-in on the server has ended, so new work waits. Ask whoever runs your ordin server to sign in again." It never asks for the sign-in in the chat, and never names a technical way in (§12 item 1). There is no warning beforehand, because Claude Code reports no expiry time (decision 50).

### 5.24 Runs that start from the chat and from events (ordin server)

Source: ADR-074, R-64, R-65, UX-A48.

- **Starting a workflow from the chat.** **/run** alone shows the workspace's workflows as buttons; **/run Invoice check** starts that one. Its inputs are asked one at a time and the run starts on the workflow's verb, as in §5.23.
- **Turning a trigger on.** Asked in words, Ordin answers with a trigger card: "Run **Invoice check** when an *invoice created* event arrives for Northwind Group, with the event's PDF link as *Invoice*. One at a time, up to 20 a day." Buttons: **Turn on**, **Not now**, **Show details** (the subject, the filter and each input's field). A subject the company has not allowed is named as such: "Your company hasn't allowed ordin to listen for these events. IT can add them." with **Send to IT**.
- **When a trigger starts a run**, one message says so: "Started **Invoice check** · invoice created, INV-2291 · **Stop**". Approval cards in that run begin with "Started by an event:". Nothing else is sent for skipped or duplicate events.
- **When events are missed or refused**, one message per trigger per day says how many and why, in words: "12 invoice events couldn't be read today, so nothing started for them."
- **The daily limit** is named when it is reached: "20 runs started from events today, the most allowed. Events will start runs again after midnight." with **Turn off this trigger** for the busiest one.
- **/triggers** lists each trigger in one line: name, on, off or "paused until midnight" (once the daily limit is reached), last event, today's count, and "Not connected" when NATS cannot be reached; each line has **Turn off** or **Turn on**.
- **Words.** The person sees "event" and the event's plain name, never the subject, consumer or stream, except under **Show details**.

### 5.25 Telegram for agents

Source: ADR-075, R-66, UX-A49.

- **Linking a chat.** **Add to this workspace › Telegram** lists the groups and channels the server's bot has been added to, with "Add ordin's bot to a Telegram group to see it here." A linked chat appears in the workspace's **Telegram** group with "Messages from 18 October, when ordin joined."
- **Telegram in the tree.** The workspace's **Telegram** group, after **Email** (§4), lists each linked chat with its count of new messages. Opening one shows a Telegram list as a screen (§5.15): each message with its sender, time and text, under the line "Messages from [date], when ordin joined." On a message, **Reply with Ordin** starts a reply, which is posted only from the post card (below); on a file, **Save file** saves it in the workspace's folder, and Ordin says "Saved “[file]” in Supplier quotes, this workspace's folder. It was checked first: no hidden content." A linked chat's **Remove [chat]…** uses the confirmation of §5.5.
- **Messages are not instructions.** A message that asks the agent to do something carries an inline note: "This message asks the agent to [what]. Messages are read as content, never as instructions, and nothing said in a group can approve anything, so nothing was done." (as for email, §5.22).
- **Granting access** reads as for email (§5.22): "Let Supplier analyst read and draft posts for Suppliers — Northwind in this workspace? It can't post: every post waits for you." **Allow read and draft** · **Read only** · **Not now**. The grants these give are named **Read and draft Telegram** and **Read Telegram**.
- **The post card** (in the person's own chat with ordin): "Post in **Suppliers — Northwind** · seen by about 14 people · from your ordin bot", the whole text, each file by name, "Replying to Ahmed, 10:31", "Drafted by Supplier analyst", then **Post**, **Not now**, **Show details**. After **Post** it reads "Posted 10:44".
- **Words.** "Post" for sending to a group or channel, never "send a message", which is the person's own chat with ordin.
- **On the desktop (UX-A51, ADR-077, UX-A69).** Telegram appears only when the Telegram app is on the computer; otherwise **Add to this workspace › Telegram** reads "Telegram isn't on this computer. Install it to link Telegram groups here." **Set up Telegram** is one screen with three numbered steps, once per computer, which anyone can follow with no IT involved (ordin is open source; no step waits on a company): (1) *Make your bot*: "BotFather is Telegram's own helper for making bots. In the chat that opens, send /newbot, then a name.", with **Open BotFather in Telegram**; (2) *Paste the key BotFather sent you*: the key is checked as it is pasted ("This is the key of your bot “[name]”" or "That isn't a bot key yet…"), kept in this computer's keychain, then **Connect**; (3) *Add your bot to a group*: **Add to a group** opens Telegram's own group picker with the bot added as an administrator, so it reads the group with no privacy setting to change. When a bot was added as an ordinary member, **Add to this workspace › Telegram** says "ordin's bot is in [group] as an ordinary member, so it sees only messages addressed to it." with **Add it as administrator**. *Amended in revision 0.44.0 by the user's decision of January 2026: this replaces the four steps of revision 0.22.0, whose fourth step (/setprivacy in BotFather) is gone; an administrator bot reads every message (ADR-075, C-55). That Telegram's add-to-group link can make the bot an administrator in one step is to be verified (§13, UX-A69).* The post card is the ordinary approval card, opened on its own screen with a one-line card in the conversation (§5.5, UX-A53). *Amended in revision 0.36.0: this said "in the conversation", which UX-A53 replaced in revision 0.24.0.* A linked chat notes that messages sent while ordin was closed for more than a day may be missing.
- **Settings › Telegram** opens with what is fixed: "Every post is shown to you before it is posted. This can't be turned off." Then the bot, with its state, and **Remove Telegram**, which acts at once (§5.5), and the chats linked in each workspace with the agents allowed there.

### 5.25a Slack for agents and handovers

- **Linking a channel.** **Add to this workspace › Slack** lists the channels the person belongs to, private ones marked, with "Only channels you're in are listed." A linked channel appears in the workspace's **Slack** group with its new-message count and "Messages from 21 October, when you linked it."

The handover draft offers **Post handover notices in #[name] without asking…** for its stage's channel, and a linked channel's screen offers **Ordin answers in #[name] without asking…**; each opens a confirmation naming the kind, the channel and the daily limit: "Post notices of [stage] handovers in #[name] as you, without asking, up to 20 a day? ### 5.26 Making and publishing an agent

Source: ADR-081 to ADR-089, sequences *Make an agent with the agent maker* and *Publish an agent through a pull request*. Prototype group "§5.26 Making an agent".

- **Ordin looks before it makes (UX-A54).** When nothing in the library covers a request, Ordin first says what the library does cover ("Contract clause checker finds clauses, but nothing checks quotes against the purchasing policy"), then offers **Make an agent**, with the closest agent as the other choice. *From revision 0.33.0 (ADR-088)* the comparison is shown as a short list headed "To do this, an agent needs to:", one line per requirement, each marked **Covered**, **Partly covered** or **Not covered**, with the agent that covers it named in plain words. The list is Ordin's wording of the request: the person can change, add or remove a line before Ordin looks again, and the marks come from ordin's own rules, not from Ordin. When the library on this computer is out of date, a line under the list says when it was last updated. The person can also ask ("make an agent"), or choose **Make an agent…** in the library. There is no blank form for a new agent.
- **The agent maker talks like a colleague.** It asks one question at a time, each answerable with a button or in the person's own words: what it should do, what it must never do. It says when other people asked for something related ("14 people asked this month…"), and when part of the work needs different access and belongs in another agent. The banner says "Talking to the agent maker"; **Back to Ordin** ends it. **Talk to the agent maker**, on the draft, takes the conversation back to it, with the draft as its topic.
- **The draft is a screen** titled "Agent draft: [name]", in this order:
  - a band saying where it stands: *Draft — only you can see it*, *Waiting for review on GitHub*, *Changes asked for*, *Approved on GitHub — not signed yet* or *Published*;
  - its purpose in one sentence, and where it came from;
  - **Checked by ordin**, "Fixed rules, the same answer every time. No agent decides these." Each check is passed or failed, with a label as well as an icon. A failed one says why in one sentence, with its fix as a button ("Leave Slack out", "Ask IT about Slack"). **ordin tidied N things in the wording** opens the list. *From revision 0.33.0* two more checks can fail (ADR-088, ADR-089):
    - **Parts that disagree.** "Two parts of this agent say different things about [what]: [part] and [part]", with **Ask the agent maker to fix this**. No agent with parts that disagree is tried or published.
    - **A choice the workspace may not answer.** "Choose which [kind of practice, in plain words] this agent uses when the workspace doesn't say", with the choices as buttons and **Let the agent ask** as the last one;
  - **Rehearsal**, which waits until every check passes;
  - **What it is**: jobs it does (each with where the job came from), will, won't, needs, and what it is built from ("Reused", "Reused and extended", "New, written for this agent"), taken from ordin's comparison of the agent maker's write-up with the library (ADR-088);
  - at its foot, **Talk to the agent maker** and **Discard**. **Discard** changes only this computer, so it uses a confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*): "Discard the draft “[name]”?", with "The draft and its rehearsal results are deleted. Nothing was published, so nobody else is affected.", **Discard** and **Cancel**.
- **An imported draft.** In a workspace that links code, **Import an agent package…** on the library (§5.2) reads an APM package into a draft in the ordin format. Its *where it came from* line names the package, and *Checked by ordin* lists anything that did not carry across as its own failed check ("[what] didn't carry across from the package"), with its fix, apart from any check about IT approval; the same rehearsal and review apply. Business users never see this route. *Amended in revision 0.39.0 (ADR-094): **Import…** on the library is for everyone and reads agents made in ordin, APM packages, and Claude Code and Copilot set-ups (§5.30).* An agent from an APM-format agent repository needs no import: it is added like any other (§5.2, ADR-087).
- **Rehearsal results.**
  - They read "4 of 5 situations passed".
  - Each situation opens to its expectations, passed or failed. A failure shows what was expected and what came back.
  - The reviewing agent's opinion follows, always introduced as "Reviewing agent's opinion — from a model, so it passes or fails nothing".
  - A failure offers **Ask the agent maker to fix this**. A changed draft says "rehearse again".
- **Trying and publishing wait for every check and the whole rehearsal.** Until then the buttons are there but unavailable, and one line says why. **Try it here — only you** adds it under *Agents*, marked "Only you — not reviewed yet".
- **Publishing.**
  - *Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).* **Next.** A bar under the draft's band always shows the one next action, as its primary button — **Fix [N] checks**, **Rehearse**, **Try it here — only you** or **Publish…** — with how many checks remain, and stays in view while the page scrolls. *Checked by ordin* lists failed checks and folds the passed ones into "[N] passed".
  - **Publish to**, above **Publish…**, lists the agent repositories the person can send a draft for review in, by display name, those the company names first (UX-A56, ADR-086).
  - **Publish…** opens an approval card (UX-A53). Its *Where* line reads "Agent repository — [display name], on GitHub"; *Affects* lists what goes for review (the agent, the results of ordin's checks and the rehearsal, and, from revision 0.34.0, which parts a model drafted, ADR-090); *Changes* says who reviews it. Its first button is **See the files**, because the files leave the computer; its approving button is **Send for review**.
  - **What See the files shows.** To a business user it shows "The agent, as the reviewers will see it:", the agent in plain words, never its files. In a workspace that links code it shows "The agent's files, written by ordin from the draft:" (§5.11). For a new agent, both also list **Who wrote each part**: "Listed in the review, so the reviewers see what a model wrote:".
  - **A repository that has nothing to start from (ADR-088).** When the chosen agent repository lacks the files every agent there needs, the card's *Affects* adds one line, "Also adds the files this agent repository needs to start", and **See the files** lists them. They go to the same review as the agent.
  - **No permission to publish.** When the person cannot send a review to any agent repository, **Publish to** is not shown, **Publish…** is unavailable, and one line says why: "None of your agent repositories lets you publish. The draft stays yours." Nothing is sent. If permission ends between choosing and sending, the card's result says so in the same words, and the draft stays the person's (ADR-084).
  - **A format that cannot hold the whole draft (ADR-087).** When the chosen repository's format cannot hold part of the draft, *Checked by ordin* shows one failed check naming what cannot be written, in plain words ("APM format can't hold [what], so it would be left out"), with two fixes as buttons: **Leave it out** and **Publish to an ordin-format repository**. **Publish…** stays unavailable until the check passes. Nothing is dropped without the person seeing it.
  - The review happens on GitHub, and is called "the review on GitHub"; **Open the review on GitHub** opens it in the browser.
  - A requested change reaches the draft and *Needs you* with the reviewer's own words, and **Ask the agent maker to make this change**. **Send the change…** opens a new card.
  - When the reviewers merge it, Ordin says it is published, and the agent appears in the library.
  - **Signed changes only (ADR-084).** When the company accepts changes to an agent repository only when they are signed on GitHub, and the reviewers approved one unsigned, the band reads *Approved on GitHub — not signed yet*, with "Your company accepts changes to [repository] only when they are signed on GitHub, and this one wasn't. The reviewers can sign it there; ordin publishes it as soon as they do." and **Open the review on GitHub**. Nothing else is asked of the person; the agent is published once the change is signed.
  - When the review is closed without merging, the draft says so with the reviewer's words and stays a draft, the person's own.
- **When something fails.** The making of the draft or a rehearsal run that fails says so in one sentence, keeps the person's answers, and offers **Try again** (sequence *Make an agent*).
- **Never shown to a business user:** files, identifiers, repository addresses, the format's technical names, versions beyond "version 0.1.0" on the draft, branches, or the words *pull request*; the person sees "the review on GitHub" (§3). In a workspace that links code, the draft also shows **The agent's files, written by ordin** (§5.11).

### 5.27 Managing an agent repository

Source: ADR-091, ADR-084, ADR-086, ADR-090; sequences *Change a part in an agent repository* and *Withdraw a part, with its replacement*. Added in revision 0.37.0 (UX-A60). Prototype group "§5.27 Managing an agent repository" (revision 0.37.3).

- **Who sees it.** Only people who can send a review to at least one agent repository (§5.26 *Publish to*). They open it from **Show its parts** on such a repository in Settings › Agent repositories, or from **Parts of an agent repository** on the agent library, beside **Make an agent…**. Everyone else sees neither entry, and no screen tells them what they are missing. Settings is never the path to finish a task (§4), so the screen opens in the centre of the open workspace as a screen tab, like the agent library (UX-A15). It is a fixed screen of the application (§5.15).
- **The screen** is titled "Parts of [display name]". **Agent repository:** at its top switches to another repository the person can send a review to; the bundled catalogue is not listed, because it changes only with ordin. Five tabs: **Parts**, **Words**, **Writing guides**, **Layout** and **Health**.
- **Plain words for a business user.** A business user sees each part by its plain name and its kind in plain words, never files, identifiers, versions, kind names, directories or the word "vocabulary" (as on the agent draft, §5.26). In a workspace that links code, the same screen also shows each part's kind, identifier, version and file, and **See the change** shows the change as a difference between files (§5.11). The kinds read as follows:

  | Shown to a business user as | Kinds |
  |---|---|
  | **agent** | WorkspaceProfile |
  | **role** | SystemRole |
  | **rule** | Rule |
  | **working mode** | OperatingMode |
  | **ability** | Skill |
  | **group of abilities** | Capability |
  | **task** | Command |
  | **reference material** | ContextResource |
  | **tool** | ToolBinding |
  | **memory rules** | Memory |
  | **way of working** | ControlLoop |
  | **working practice** | PracticePack |
  | **language**, **writing style**, **code workflow**, **hand-over** | LanguageOverlay, StyleOverlay, SourceWorkflowOverlay, HandoffOverlay |
  | **rehearsal checks** | Evaluation |
  | **what a result must hold** | OutputContract |
  | **words** (the **Words** tab) | Vocabulary |
  | **writing guide** (the **Writing guides** tab) | Guideline |
  | **lifecycle** (*from revision 0.38.0, ADR-092*) | Journey |
  | not shown to a business user; listed as "Read, not offered" for people who code | CrewProfile, AgentIdentity, BrandOverlay, JurisdictionOverlay |

- **Parts.** A search box, a choice of kind in plain words, and **Show: In use · Not for new agents · Withdrawn**. Each row shows the part's name, its kind, one sentence of what it does, "Used by [N] agents", and its state as a label with an icon. *Amended in revision 0.40.0 (UX-A64, ADR-095): each row is one line, its name, its kind and its state; what it does and "Used by [N] agents" are in the row's accessible description and on the chosen part. In a workspace that links code no identifier or version is added to the row.* **New part…** at the top asks for the kind (rule, ability, working practice, writing guide, memory rules, way of working or, from revision 0.38.0, lifecycle; *from revision 0.39.0 every kind a person can write, grouped as in §5.30, ADR-094*) and a name, then opens a part draft. Choosing a part shows, in this order:
  - what it does, in one or two sentences;
  - **Used by**: the agents that use it, by plain name, each with its "From:" line, and the parts that use it; "Not used by any agent" when there are none;
  - **History of this part**: "Replaces: [part]" and "Replaced by: [part]" where they apply, and, in a workspace that links code, its versions with their dates;
  - for a working practice or an overlay, **Fits**: the agents it says it fits and the agents that offer it. A disagreement reads "[agent] offers it, but it says it doesn't fit [agent]", with **Change who it fits…** (a part draft) and **Change what [agent] offers…**, which opens the agent as a draft with the agent maker (§5.26);
  - its actions: **Read it** (*from revision 0.39.0, ADR-093: the part page of §5.29, for every kind*), **Change…** (only for the six kinds **New part…** offers), **Stop using in new agents…**, **Withdraw…** and **Ask Ordin about this part**.

   *Amended in revision 0.40.0 (UX-A64, ADR-095): the chosen part shows what it does and two buttons, **Read it** and **Ask Ordin about this part**; **Used by**, **History of this part**, **Fits** and, in a workspace that links code, **Details** (kind, identifier, version, file and versions) are folded sections, each heading with its count where it has one ("Used by · 3 agents"); **Change…**, **Stop using in new agents…** and **Withdraw…** are in **More**.* *Amended in revision 0.44.0 (UX-A70).* **Change…** sits beside **Read it**, for people who can send a review and a kind a part draft writes; **More** keeps **Stop using in new agents…** and **Withdraw…**. The choice of kind lists the kinds in the four groups **New part…** uses (What it knows and does, How it behaves, How work is done here, How it is checked), then *Read, not offered*.
- **The part draft** is a screen titled "Part draft: [name]", laid out like the agent draft (§5.26):
  - the same band: *Draft — only you can see it*, *Waiting for review on GitHub*, *Changes asked for*, *Approved on GitHub — not signed yet* or *Published*;
  - **Used by** first: "Changing it changes [N] agents once it's published: [agent], [agent]…";
  - **Checked by ordin**, with the same heading line and the same pass or fail with a label and an icon. One check is always there, "Checked with the whole of [display name]"; when an agent that uses the part would no longer pass, the failure names the agent and why in one sentence;
  - the part's form, with **How to write [a kind]** beside it, taken from the repository's writing guide for that kind;
  - **Suggest wording**, whose text is marked "Suggested by a model" until the person changes it, and is listed in the review as model-drafted (ADR-090);
  - one question with choices before **Publish…**: "Does this change what the part does?" with **No, it's a correction** and **Yes**. A business user never sees the version this decides; in a workspace that links code the line reads "Version [old] → [new]";
  - on a new part, an optional **Replaces** field: "Does it replace an older part?";
  - at its foot, **Discard**, with a confirmation (§5.5 *Confirmations*): "Discard the part draft “[name]”?", with "Nothing was published, so nobody else is affected.", **Discard** and **Cancel**.
- **Publishing a part draft** follows §5.26 *Publishing*. **Publish…** waits until every check passes, and opens an approval card (UX-A53):
  - *Where:* "Agent repository — [display name], on GitHub";
  - *Affects:* "[part], and the [N] agents that use it";
  - *Changes:* "Reviewed on GitHub by [the reviewers ordin can read]". Where the repository asks for no review: "[display name] asks for no review, so this can be merged without one.";
  - first button **See the change**; approving button **Send for review**.

  The draft and *Needs you* then follow the review as an agent draft does, with the same notices (§7). A change asked for has **Change the draft**, then **Send the change…** with a new card.
- **Stop using in new agents…** opens a part draft that holds only that change, with "[N] agents use it. They keep working; new agents won't use it." and an optional **Replaced by**. Its card's *Changes* reads "[part] stays in the agents that use it, and isn't offered for new ones." *From revision 0.37.4 (UI-D100) the card also names the reviewers, as every part card does (ADR-091 item 11).*
- **Withdraw…** opens a screen, "Withdraw [part]":
  1. **Used by** first: "These agents stop on every computer that uses them once this is published:", the list.
  2. "Why is it withdrawn?", required, in the person's words; people whose agents stop read it.
  3. **Replace it with** (optional): parts of the same kind. With one chosen, the list splits into "Move to [replacement]" and "Stop", from ordin's checks.
  4. **Checked by ordin**: the part exists and is not withdrawn yet, the replacement is usable, and each agent that moves passes with it.
  5. **Withdraw…** opens the approval card: *Where* as above; *Affects:* "The list of withdrawn parts of [display name], and the [N] agents that use [part]"; *Changes:* "[part] stops working everywhere once the reviewers merge it. This can't be undone; a new part can replace it."; first button **See what will be sent**; approving button **Send for review**, never the default button (UX-A2).
- **Words.** One list per kind of word, in plain words: **Kinds of working practice**, **Areas**, **Who it's for**, **Kinds of rule**, **States of a part**, **What an agent may remember**, **Ways of working** and **What a way of working needs**. Each word shows its label, its description and "Used by [N] parts". **Add a word…** and **Retire…** each open a part draft. **Retire…** on a word that parts of this repository still use fails its check with "[N] parts still use this word:" and their names. Where parts in another agent repository the person reads use the same word, the card says so as a warning: "[other repository] also uses this word. It keeps its own list, so it is not changed." **States of a part** reads "Fixed by ordin", with no **Retire…**.
- **Writing guides.** One per kind, each with "Changed only after a review by: [reviewers]" where ordin can read who they are (C-67). **Change…** opens a part draft. Under each guide, **What rehearsals check for [kind]** lists each check as **Decides** or **Opinion only, from a model**; it is read only here, and people who code see "Changed in the repository itself".
- **Layout.** A business user sees the kinds in plain words, how many parts of each the repository holds, and one line on what each is for. In a workspace that links code it adds each kind's folder, the identifier pattern with the categories in use, and each folder's authoring guide, opened as a Markdown page to read. **Layout** shows; it moves nothing.
- **Health.** "Checked [time], after the last update." Groups, each with its count, and each row opening its part: **Not used by any agent**, **Look alike**, **Same name in two repositories** (people who code see it as **Same identifier in two repositories**, with the identifier; UI-D101), **Uses a part that can't be found**, **Used while not for new agents**, **Used while withdrawn**, **Words no part uses**. The tab's line reads "Health suggests; it decides nothing." Its count shows on the tab.
- **An APM-format repository.** Only **Parts** is shown, with **Used by**, **Withdraw…** and **Ask Ordin about this part** (UI-D102); one line says "[display name] is in APM format, so ordin can withdraw parts from it but not change them."
- **Never shown to a business user:** files, identifiers, versions, kind names, folders, repository addresses, branches, the name of the withdrawn list's file, or the words *pull request*, *vocabulary* and *deprecated* (§3).

### 5.28 Handing work over

Source: ADR-092, ADR-090, ADR-091; sequences *Hand work over* and *Receive a handover*. Added in revision 0.38.0 (UX-A61). Prototype group "§5.28 Handing work over".

People in a lifecycle, such as software delivery, pass their work to the next person, who continues from it in their own ordin. A handover carries the results, where they live, and a note; it travels through a place both people already share, and ordin runs nothing between them.

- **A workspace that follows a stage.** Its header reads "[Lifecycle] · [stage]", with "Next: [next stage]" and **Show the lifecycle**. A workspace follows a stage when it was started from a handover, when the person chooses one with **Follow a stage…** among the workspace's actions in the tree (**Change the stage it follows…** once it follows one, with **Stop following it**; *amended in revision 0.38.1, UI-D105: workspaces have no settings screen*), or when Ordin asks on adding the agent a stage names: "Supplier analyst does the Analysis stage of Supplier onboarding. Should this workspace follow it?" with **Follow it** and **Not now**.
- **Offering a handover.** When a task or crew in a workspace that follows a stage finishes and its result is what the stage hands over, Ordin says so in one sentence and offers it: "The supplier assessment is ready for the Contracting stage. Hand it over?" with **Hand over…** and **Not yet**. **Hand over…** is also an action of every workspace and every document, in the tree and on the document page, inside a lifecycle or not. Outside a lifecycle the draft suggests no one and checks no stage's needs, and **Where it goes:** is a choice.
- **The handover draft** is a screen titled "Handover: [title]", on the full-pane page (UX-A59), laid out like the agent draft (§5.26): *Amended in revision 0.50.0 (ADR-102): under **To:**, **Also tell them in Slack**, ticked when the receiver is found in Slack, or "Not found in Slack — they'll get an email"; and, for a stage that names a Slack channel, "Also posted in #[name] as you" (with "— your rule" when a rule covers it).*
  - the band: *Draft — only you can see it*, then the states below;
  - *Added in revision 0.45.0 (ADR-099, UX-A71).* **Which stage comes next?**, first, when the stage leads to a choice: each choice with its *when* ("Risk review — The supplier is rated high risk"), Ordin's recommendation first and marked, with its reason ("I recommend: Risk review — Kestrel supplier assessment rates Kestrel as high risk."), and "[lifecycle] leaves this choice to you. Ordin recommends from what the result says; no agent chooses." Until a stage is chosen, **To:** reads "Choose the next stage first; ordin then suggests who takes it." and the only check is "The next stage is chosen";
  - **To:** one person, with a search over the company directory, or over the people who can review in the repository when the handover place is Git; *amended in revision 0.54.0 (ADR-106): or over the people who can be assigned issues in the project when the handover place is Jira, where no address is typed;* in a stage that has handed over before, the person it last went to is suggested; *Added in revision 0.45.0 (ADR-099, UX-A71).* when the stage leads to stages side by side, one **To:** per stage, each labelled with its stage, and the line "Side by side: each gets their own handover, at once. [stage] starts when both have handed over."; the card names every receiver and stage;
  - **What's handed over**: the results, each with where it lives, and **Add a result…**;
  - **The note**, with four headings, **What's done**, **Decisions made**, **Open questions** and **What's needed next**, drafted by Ordin from the workspace and marked "Drafted by Ordin" until the person changes it; edited with the formatting toolbar;
  - **Checked by ordin**, with the same heading line, pass or fail, label and icon as the agent draft: "Every result is somewhere [name] can read" (failing with **Put it in [place]…**), "[result] holds everything the [stage] stage needs" when the stage asks for it (failing with "It has no [section]." and **Ask Ordin to add it**), and "[name] can be reached";
  - **Where it goes:** "Through [place]" in plain words ("Through Supplier onboarding in SharePoint", "Through supplier-portal on GitHub"); outside a lifecycle, a choice among the places the workspace links; *amended in revision 0.54.0 (ADR-106; UX-A75): for a Jira project, "Through ABC-12 in Jira", the issue the workspace links in that project; when it links several, **Which issue?** lists them, the most recently linked first; when it links none, "A new issue will be created in [project]", with **Use an existing issue…**, which opens the Jira issue search;*
  - at its foot, **Hand over…**, unavailable with the reason until every check passes, and **Discard**, with a confirmation (§5.5): "Discard the handover to [name]?".
- **The card.** **Hand over…** opens an approval card (UX-A53): *Where:* "[place]"; *Affects:* "[name]", marked "Outside your company" when they are (§5.22); *Changes:* "[name] is asked to take this over. They see the note and the results; nothing else in this workspace." When the receiver does not use ordin, a second line: "[name] doesn't use ordin, so they'll get an email from you with the note and links." *Amended in revision 0.54.0 (ADR-106; UX-A75): for a Jira place, *Where:* "ABC-12 in Jira" or "A new issue in [project], named [title]"; *Changes:* "ABC-12 gets the note and the results as comments and is assigned to [name]. Its status doesn't change."; no email line, because Jira tells the receiver itself.* First button **See what will be sent**; approving button **Hand over**. *Amended in revision 0.50.0: the card also lists each Slack post it causes — "Slack: a message to [name] from you", "Slack: #[name], as you" — with its text under **See what will be sent**.*
- **The sender's side, after.** The draft's band follows the state: *Waiting for [name]*, *Seen by [name]*, *Accepted by [name]*, *Returned by [name]*, with their reason. **Take it back…** (a confirmation) is offered until it is accepted. When a stage sets days to answer and they pass, Home's *Waiting for you* has "No answer from [name] in [N] days" with **Remind [name]…**, an email draft the person approves. *Amended in revision 0.50.0: when the stage names a Slack channel, **Remind [name]…** opens a card for a post in that channel mentioning [name], or posts under the stage's rule, in place of the email draft.*
- **Receiving.** "Handed to you: [title] — from [name]" appears in *Needs you* and in Home's *Waiting for you*, with a notice outside the window (§7). It opens the **handover screen**, titled "Handed to you: [title]": *Amended in revision 0.50.0: **Open in ordin** in a Slack notice or email opens this screen; when the handover's place is not yet followed, ordin starts following it and says "ordin now checks [place] for handovers."*
  - "From [name], [time]", and the lifecycle and stage when there is one: "Supplier onboarding · Contracting, after Analysis";
  - the note, read as a formatted page;
  - **What's handed over**: each result, opening where it lives;
  - **Checked by [name]'s ordin**: the checks that passed, read only;
  - **Accept and start**, **Return to [name]…** and **Ask Ordin about this**.
- **Accept and start** asks one question: "Start a new workspace for the [stage] stage, or use one you have?" with **New workspace** (named from the title) and a list of the person's workspaces. The workspace then follows the stage, links the results as inputs, and offers the stage's agent with **Add**, asking before anything is installed (§5.2). Ordin's first message uses the note: "[name] handed over the supplier assessment. Two questions are open: [first], [second]. Shall I start with the contract draft?"
- *Added in revision 0.45.0 (ADR-099, UX-A71).* **Where stages meet.** When the handover is one of several sent side by side, the handover screen opens with "1 of 2 handed over. [stage] and [stage] were sent side by side from [stage]. [this stage] starts when [stage] has handed over too; you can accept this one now."
- **Return to [name]…** opens a short form: "Why are you returning it?", required, in the person's words; then an approval card: *Affects:* "[name]"; *Changes:* "[name] gets it back with your reason. Nothing you changed is sent." The sender sees "Returned to you: [title] — by [name]" in *Waiting for you*, with the reason, and **Change and hand over again…**, which reopens the draft as its next round.
- **Words from another person.** The note and results are shown as from the sender. Agents read them as inputs; a line in the note that tries to instruct an agent or ordin is marked inline, "This line asks an agent to do something. Agents won't follow it.", as on a Telegram message (§5.25).
- **In the tree** (*from revision 0.39.2*). A workspace that follows a stage, or has handovers, has its own group between *Workflows* and *Dashboards*, named after the lifecycle ("Supplier onboarding"), or **Handovers** when it follows none. It holds, in this order: one row for the stage, "[stage] · next: [next stage]", opening the lifecycle screen; **Handed to you**, one row per handover received, with its state ("Waiting for you", "Accepted by you", "Returned by you"); **Handed over**, one row per handover sent, with its state. The group's count is what waits for the person: handovers to answer and handovers returned. Its actions are **Hand over…**, **Follow a stage…** (**Change the stage it follows…**) and **Show the lifecycle**. The tree stays text; the picture is on the lifecycle screen.
- **Starting a workspace for a stage** (*from revision 0.40.0, ADR-095*). **Start a workspace for this stage…** is on each stage of the lifecycle screen, in the path and in the list; **New workspace** offers **For a stage of a lifecycle**, then the lifecycle and the stage; and the person can ask Ordin ("Set me up for Contracting in Supplier onboarding"). Each opens one screen, "Start a workspace for [stage]":
  - **Name**, filled in as "[lifecycle] — [stage]" and changeable;
  - **What happens**, in plain words: "It follows the [stage] stage of [lifecycle].", "Handovers for this stage come through [place].", and, when the stage names an agent, "[agent] is offered; nothing is installed until you add it.";
  - **What this stage hands on**, the sections the stage's result must hold, as a plain list read from its requirements, with nothing to tick: only ordin's check of the result marks a part found; "This stage names no requirements." when it has none;
  - **Start** and **Cancel**.

  **Start** creates the workspace, makes it follow the stage, links the place and opens it; Ordin's first message names the stage and offers the agent with **Add** (§5.2). The list stays on the stage row's page in the tree as **What this stage hands on**, each item marked found by ordin's check of the result as the work goes (ADR-092 item 2); a part not found yet shows a dash, never an empty box the person could take for a control.
- **The lifecycle draft** (*from revision 0.40.0, ADR-095*). **New part…** with the kind lifecycle, **Change…** on a lifecycle, and **Make a lifecycle…** open a draft titled "Lifecycle draft: [name]", laid out like the part draft (§5.27), with the same band, *Checked by ordin*, approval card and review on GitHub:
  - **Who follows it** first, as **Used by** comes first on a part draft: the workspaces on this computer that follow the lifecycle and the handovers still open in it;
  - **The stages**, drawn as the path of the lifecycle screen, with **Add a stage**, **Move earlier**, **Move later** and **Remove…** on each; choosing a stage opens its form: **Name**, **Role** (plain words), **Agent** (from the library, optional), **Hands on** (a "what a result must hold" part of the repository, or **Write one…**, a part draft of that kind), **Handovers go through** (a Git repository or SharePoint folder the person can read), and **Days to answer** (optional);
  - **Checked by ordin**: every stage has a name and a role; each agent named can be found; each "hands on" part exists; each place can be reached by the person; and, where the next stage's agent declares what it takes, a stage hands on what the next stage takes, each failure named in plain words with its fix;
  - **Publish…** opens the approval card, whose *Affects* line names the workspaces on this computer that follow the lifecycle and any handover still open in it ("2 workspaces follow it; 1 handover is still open"), and whose *Changes* line says that stages removed or renamed are shown to people at their next handover;
  - **The review on GitHub** (*from revision 0.41.0*), as for a part draft (§5.27): when the reviewers ask for changes, the band reads *Changes asked for* with the reviewer's words, *Needs you* lists "Changes asked for: [name]" and the notice of §7 follows; **Change the draft** makes the stages and their forms editable again, *Checked by ordin* runs on every change, and **Send the change…** opens a new approval card ("Send the change to "[name]" for review?") whose *Affects* line names the review already open and whose *Changes* line says the reviewers see the change in the same review; when they approve it, the band reads *Published*.
  - **Make a lifecycle…**, beside **Make a part…** on the agent library for people who can send a review, starts the agent maker: "Which piece of work is this, and who does it?", then it proposes the stages one question at a time; its text is marked "Suggested by a model" until the person changes it (ADR-090).

### 5.30 Importing, checking and fixing

Source: ADR-094, ADR-087, ADR-091, ADR-093; sequence *Import an agent or a part, check it and fix it*. Added in revision 0.39.0 (UX-A63). Prototype group "§5.30 Importing, checking and fixing".

- **Import…** is on the agent library, beside **Make an agent…**, for everyone. It asks "What are you bringing in?" with **An agent** and **One part**, then the file, folder or package. On a file or folder in a workspace that ordin recognises, the tree offers **Import as an agent…** or **Import as a part…**.
- **What ordin reads,** in plain words on the import screen: "Agents made in ordin, APM packages, and Claude Code and GitHub Copilot set-ups." A business user never sees file names of the set-up; in a workspace that links code, the import screen lists each file read and what it became.
- **An imported agent** opens as an agent draft (§5.26), with *where it came from* reading "Imported from [source], [date]" ("Imported from a Claude Code set-up in supplier-portal, 19 Oct"), **What it's made of** filled from the import (§5.29), and *Checked by ordin*. Anything that has no place in an ordin agent is a failed check: "[what] has no place in an ordin agent", with **Leave it out** and **Ask Ordin to fix it**.
- **An imported part.** ordin says what it reads like, in one sentence: "This reads like an ability: steps an agent follows to check a quote." with **Yes** and a choice of other kinds. Then **Which agent repository is it for?**, among those the person can send a review to, and the part draft opens (§5.27).
- **Check it** is on **What it's made of** and on every part page. It shows *Checked by ordin* in place, with "Checked [time], against the version in use", and one line per finding naming the part. With nothing found: "Every check passed."
- **Fixes.** Each failed check offers, in this order, those that apply:
  - **Fix it**, when ordin knows the one right answer; the line says what it will do: "Point it to Reading notice periods, in days or weeks, which replaced it";
  - **Ask Ordin to fix it**, when judgement is needed; the text it suggests is marked "Suggested by a model" until the person changes it;
  - **Change it yourself**.

  A fix on a library agent or part opens its draft with the fix applied, and the band says "Fixed here; nothing changes for anyone until it is published." On an installed agent whose repository the person cannot send a review to, the fix makes **your own fixed copy**: the band says "Your own fixed copy — only on this computer", **Try it** works as on any draft, and **Suggest the fix to [display name]…** opens an approval card whose *Changes* line reads "The maintainers of [display name] are asked to take your fix. Your copy keeps working until they do."
- **New part…** and **Make a part….** **New part…** on **Parts of [repository]** offers every kind a person can write, in plain words, grouped: *What it knows and does* (ability, group of abilities, task, reference material, tool), *How it behaves* (role, rule, working mode, way of working, memory rules), *How work is done here* (working practice, language, writing style, hand-over, lifecycle, and code workflow in a workspace that links code), *How it is checked* (rehearsal checks, what a result must hold, writing guide). **Make a part…** on the agent library, beside **Make an agent…** for people who can send a review, starts the agent maker for one part: "What should this part do?", then it proposes the kind and drafts the part one question at a time.
- **Never shown to a business user:** formats' names beyond the sentence above, file names, kind names, identifiers, versions, or the words *mapping report* and *plug-in* (§3).

### 5.31 Your ordin server on the desktop

Source: ADR-096, ADR-073, ADR-076, OQ-39, R-85; sequence *Connect the desktop to an ordin server*. Added in revision 0.42.0 (UX-A66). Prototype group "§5.31 Your ordin server on the desktop".

The person's desktop can show their own ordin server beside their own work. The server's work stays on the server; the desktop is one more place to see it and answer it, as the chat is.

- **Connecting.** **Settings › Servers** lists each server with its state and **Connect to a server…**. The screen asks two things: "Its address" and "The code from whoever runs your ordin server". Where the company has no certificate authority, the person pastes the one line the server prints in place of both. **Connect** pairs the desktop. A wrong or expired code: "That code didn't work. Ask for a new one from whoever runs your ordin server." The person never types a key, a port or a certificate; an address is the only technical word, and only on this screen.
- **In the tree.** A connected server is a top-level group below the person's own workspaces, under its display name with a small server icon ("Northwind server"), holding its workspaces with their counts of what waits. Its workspaces open as the desktop's own do, with the server's name above their screens: "On Northwind server". Each has its own conversation with Ordin, because the server's Ordin is its own.
- **Needs you and Home** list the server's items beside the desktop's own, each with "On [server]" after its workspace. A server's approval card opens as any approval card does (§5.5). Once decided, the card in the chats reads "Decided on [this computer's name], 10:44", and a card decided in a chat shows "Decided in Telegram, 10:42" here, with no buttons (ADR-076).
- **What works on a server's workspace:** the conversation, forms, progress, approvals, results, documents, workflows, reports, dashboards, knowledge pages, email drafts and memory pages, with hand editing as on the desktop. Dictation works.
- **What doesn't, said where it is asked for:**
  - adding a folder on this computer: "Folders on this computer can't be added to a workspace on Northwind server.";
  - opening a document in the person's own app: **Open a copy in Word** with the line "This saves a copy on this computer. Changes to the copy stay here.";
  - the code editor, *Changes waiting* and the terminal: "Code views aren't available for workspaces on a server yet." (OQ-60).
- **Save a copy on this computer** is among a server document's actions. The copy is the desktop's own from then on.
- **Not connected.** When the link drops, the server's group in the tree and its workspaces' headers read **Not connected** with the time of the last update ("Not connected · as of 10:42"); what is shown stays visible, read-only, and buttons that would send something are disabled with the line "Waiting for Northwind server". The desktop reconnects by itself and says nothing more until it is back; then the line goes and *Needs you* catches up. The server's work goes on meanwhile, and its cards still reach the person's chats.
- **Disconnecting.** **Disconnect this computer…** in **Settings › Servers** asks "Disconnect this computer from Northwind server?" with "The server keeps working. You can connect again with a new code." (UX-A57). **Remove this server** forgets it on this computer.
- **A different version:** "Northwind server runs a different version of ordin. Ask whoever runs it to update it." or "Update ordin on this computer to use Northwind server.", whichever side is older.
- **Never shown to a business user:** port numbers, keys, certificates, the words *API*, *stream*, *tunnel* or *pairing*.

## 9. Responsiveness

Source: 07 — Cross-cutting NFRs (A-03).

- Interface actions that wait for no model respond within 150 ms at the 95th percentile. Design screens so that the first response to a click never depends on the network.
- The conversation with Ordin is interactive within 3 s of opening the application, excluding sign-in. Ordin's first reply may take longer; the person can type at once.
- A local session's progress reaches the screen within 1 s of being emitted; show it as it arrives rather than batching it.
- Anything that waits on a model or a remote agent shows its current step at once (§5.4).

## 10. Accessibility

The design states no accessibility obligation. The following are UX-A6, proposed as the baseline:

- WCAG 2.2 level AA for every view outside the code editor.
- Every action reachable by keyboard, with a visible focus indicator; approval cards fully operable by keyboard.
- A confirmation (§5.5) is a modal dialog: focus moves to its heading, Tab stays inside it, Escape cancels, and focus returns to the control that opened it; a destructive verb is never its default button (UX-A57, UX-A2).
- An invalid value in a setting, such as the number of days in Settings › History, is named in a line under the field, tied to it for screen readers, with the field keeping focus (UX-A58).
- F6 and Shift+F6 move focus between the three regions: the tree, the open screen and the conversation (UX-A15). Screen tabs and conversation tabs follow the WAI-ARIA tabs pattern: the arrow keys move between tabs and show them, and Delete closes a screen tab. Panel edges are operable by keyboard (UX-A16).
- **Search or go to…** opens with Ctrl+K (⌘K on a Mac) from anywhere in the window, and is also a rail control reachable with Tab. It follows the WAI-ARIA combobox pattern with a listbox: the arrow keys move the active result, announced with its group and workspace; Enter opens it; Escape closes the dialog and returns focus to where it was. The dialog traps focus while open and names its keys in its footer (UX-A30).
- A changed mark is part of its row's accessible name ("changed since you last looked"), and a folded folder's count too ("2 changed since you last looked"); Home's count of new items is part of the **Home** button's accessible name ("Home, 4 new") (UX-A29).
- Trees follow the WAI-ARIA tree pattern: one tab stop, the arrow keys move and fold, Enter opens, and Shift+F10 or the Menu key opens a node's actions menu, which Escape closes, returning focus to the node (UX-A14).
- Status is never carried by colour alone: run states carry a label (§8) and an icon.
- Interface text scales with the operating system's text size without loss of content.
- Screen-reader announcements for new approvals and for task completion.
- Respect the operating system's reduced-motion and light or dark appearance settings.
- Text can be entered by voice wherever it can be typed in the conversation and in form fields, through the operating system's on-device dictation (ADR-043). *From revision 0.22.0 (ADR-078) on macOS 26 and later only; on Windows and older macOS text is typed, and Settings › Dictation says so.*
- Right-to-left locales lay out mirrored, with direction-bearing icons mirrored (ADR-042).

## 11. Guidance for agents

Agents reach the person through ordin's screens, so the way an agent is written shapes the experience too. *From revision 0.27.0* agents come from agent repositories in one of two formats, each read and written by a format plug-in in ordin's own code (ADR-082, ADR-086, ADR-087):

- the **ordin format**, crucible's component model, which is ordin's own and holds everything ordin can express;
- the **APM format**, read and written so that companies already keeping agents as APM packages can use them and publish to them.

Both become the same parts inside ordin, so a task's form is built the same way whichever format the agent came in; what differs is what each format can say about a task. ordin installs the agent for the session's harness, Claude Code or Copilot (ADR-082, ADR-038). *Before revision 0.25.0 this section described APM packages only, installed through `apm` (ADR-004, superseded).*

### 11.1 What each format gives a form

**ordin format.** A task is a part of kind `command`, a Markdown file with YAML frontmatter, as in the bundled catalogue's `crucible/models/@default/commands/intent/rfc.md`:

| Field | Example | Used on screen as |
|---|---|---|
| `metadata.name` | `/rfc` | The task's name, without its leading "/" and turned into words. Agents made in ordin get task names that start with a verb, by ordin's wording rules (ADR-085). |
| `metadata.description` | "Captures a change request as a structured document. …" | The task's one-line explanation in forms and in the library, and what Ordin reads to choose the task |
| `argument-hint` | `"<paste change request text, or path to a saved file>"` | The one field's label and placeholder, with the angle brackets removed |
| the body's `$ARGUMENTS` | — | One free-text field, filled into the task when it starts |
| `invokes` | the skills and capabilities the command uses | Nothing on screen; what the agent is built from shows on its draft (§5.26). *From revision 0.39.0 (ADR-093): **Uses** on the task's page under **What it's made of** (§5.29).* |

**APM format.** A task is an APM prompt file (`.apm/prompts/<task>.prompt.md`). Its frontmatter, as AgentWright writes it today (`ff-dossier-to-agent-builder/fixtures/build/release-manager-apm/expected/.apm/prompts/run-go-no-go.prompt.md`), carries three fields:

| Frontmatter field | Example | Used on screen as |
|---|---|---|
| `description` | "Run the go/no-go decision for a release and produce the decision with its sign-offs recorded." | The task's one-line explanation in forms and in the library, and what Ordin reads to choose the task |
| `input` | `[arguments]` | One form field per named input, filled into the prompt's `${input:<name>}` placeholder |
| `argument-hint` | `<release candidate>` | The field's label and placeholder, with the angle brackets removed: "Release candidate" |

The task name comes from the prompt file's name, turned into words ("run-go-no-go" → "Run go/no-go"). The heading inside the prompt (`# /run-go-no-go — Run the go/no-go decision`) carries a friendlier name, but the heading is prose, and ordin reads agent text as data only (02 — Architecture), so the name is not taken from it.

When an APM package is read, each prompt becomes the same task the ordin format holds. Anything with no equivalent goes into the mapping report, and the agent is not offered until it carries across (ADR-087, §5.2).

### 11.2 What authors should do

For agents in either format:

- Task names are verb phrases a business user recognises: "Prepare account brief", "Review supplier responses".
- The task's description is one sentence a business user understands, saying what the task produces. It is what Ordin reads to choose an agent, so it says plainly what the task is for and what it needs.
- `argument-hint` names the input in plain words. Optional parts in square brackets, `key=value` options and file paths ("`<dossier path> [out-dir] [target=apm]`", from AgentWright's own builder commands, or "path to a saved file" in the ordin-format example above) cannot become a friendly form, so they belong in agents for workspaces with code only.
- Final messages summarise what was done and where the results are, without internal reasoning.
- **State the plan, then mark it (ADR-068).** A task that takes more than one step calls ordin's **plan** tool before its first other tool call, with three to seven steps of at most 80 characters in plain words ("Read the supplier quotes", not "Parse XLSX inputs"), and marks each step *now* and *done* as it goes. A task that changes its approach states the new plan; it does not silently rename steps.
- **Show data as a report, not as prose (ADR-067).** Numbers the person will compare go to ordin's **report** tool as a table, chart or key figures, each part with its source. The final message then says what the report shows in one or two sentences. An agent does not ship its own interface pages for ordin; they are not displayed.
- An agent's questions are written so Ordin can answer them from the request and the workspace: one question at a time, with the choices it needs. Ordin decides what reaches the person (§5.16).

For each format:

- **ordin format.** Write `metadata.description` for a business user, and keep engineering detail in the command's body. An agent made with the agent maker has these fields written by ordin from the draft, with the wording rules applied and shown as "ordin tidied N things" (ADR-085); its author checks the words on the draft (§5.26), not the files.
- **APM format.** Give each prompt file a name that reads as a verb phrase once turned into words, because the name comes from the file name (§11.1). An agent made in ordin and published to an APM-format repository keeps only what the APM format can hold; anything else fails the check of §5.26 before it can be published, so keep a task within what the APM format holds, or publish it to an ordin-format repository.

### 11.3 What neither format gives yet

Both formats give a task one free-text input: `$ARGUMENTS` in the ordin format; in the APM format one named `input`, which in the agent packages AgentWright writes today is always `arguments` (the release-manager fixture cited in §11.1), while AgentWright's own builder commands declare none and read `$ARGUMENTS`. Neither gives the task a display name of its own, or each input a label, a kind (text, document, place, choice) and whether it is required. So a form with separate fields, document and place pickers, choices and validation (§5.3) cannot be built from an agent's files alone. Until a format carries this, ordin builds the form from §11.1, and Ordin fills its one field from the conversation for the person to check.

The user decided on January 2026 that the format should carry this (UX-OQ-1, option c). Since revision 0.27.0 that is a change to the ordin format, which ordin owns (ADR-082, ADR-087), and is made in ordin rather than requested of AgentWright; it is not yet designed. The APM format gains it only if APM's own format does; until then, publishing such a task to an APM-format repository names what would be lost (§5.26).

## 12. Screen review checklist

Every screen outside engineering mode and Diagnostics, and every kind of item Ordin shows, passes each item before it is accepted. Each item is pass or fail.

1. No path, version, package name, tool name, command, address, channel identifier or error code is visible. The one exception is the web address the person pastes on **Add an agent repository…**, which is not shown again once added (ADR-086).
2. The person is asked only to sign in, choose, supply inputs, approve or reject, edit, or talk with an agent.
3. Every externally visible or destructive action goes through an approval card with all four parts of §5.5.
4. Every error is one sentence with at most one action, and a repair was attempted first.
5. Progress shows observable events; no invented percentage; no model reasoning.
6. The start-up report is shown when something is not carried across.
7. Every button label is a verb that says what happens.
8. The person can see how to stop any running work, and what stopping does.
9. Every file change is visible as a version with its origin.
10. Interface text comes from the message catalogue and is shown in the person's locale; the screen passes with the pseudo-locale (40% longer text) and the right-to-left pseudo-locale, and dates and numbers follow the locale (ADR-042).
11. The UX-A6 accessibility baseline is met.
12. The screen has been tried by at least one business user who was not involved in building it, who completed the task without help (UX-A7).
13. Anything the person can do by clicking they can also ask Ordin for, and anything asked for in words reaches the person as the same form, plan or approval card (P2, §5.12).
14. The current workspace is visible, and any list of documents stays usable at 100,000 files: tree, sortable list, breadcrumb and search, with the side-rail tree listing documents in pages (§5.6, A-21, UX-A14).
15. Every item Ordin shows is from the catalogue of §5.15, drawn by the application; no item other than an approval card carries a button that approves, sends, posts or publishes.
16. No change Ordin or an agent made can leave the computer without the approval card of §5.6, and a change at the origin since Ordin read the document blocks sending it.
17. Anything waiting for the person is in *Needs you*, not only in the conversation's scroll, and cannot be closed until it stops waiting (§4, UX-A12).
18. What Ordin says to the person is brief: the answer or decision first, with a recommendation where one exists (§5.16).
19. The screen passes with the default brand and with a test brand of a different name length, colours and fonts; no product or coordinator name, colour or logo is literal in the screen (ADR-041).
20. Choosing something to look at adds nothing to the conversation and does not move its scroll; it opens a screen. Everything that waits for the person is in the conversation in full (UX-A15).
21. Anything shown across workspaces — on Home or in the search box — names its workspace, opens there, carries no button that approves, sends, posts or publishes, and is never given to an agent of another workspace (§5.17).
22. Nothing on a screen asks the person to understand tokens, models or caches; plan use is shown as levels, bands and model turns, and model names appear only for people who code and in Diagnostics (§5.18).
23. Every message from Ordin gives the answer first, in plain words, within the person's chosen length, and in their chosen tone; no tone changes an approval card, an error, a run state or a fact (§5.19).
24. A workflow's steps read as a numbered list in plain words, with the agent and what each step uses; every change to a workflow, from its editor, from Ordin or from a file, is shown before it is saved (ADR-064).
25. Every block in a workflow reads as a sentence with its maximum and who decides it; every path a run took is named on its progress with why; a shared workflow says where it lives and never changes without the person choosing (ADR-065, ADR-066).
26. Every table, chart and key figure says where its numbers come from and "as of" when; every chart has **Show as table** and does not rely on colour alone; a dashboard refreshes only when the person asks (ADR-067).
27. Every progress screen leads with one Now sentence; steps carry a symbol and a word; a step count comes from a known list of steps; a changed plan is announced; a step claimed done with no activity seen says so; there is no percentage bar or time remaining (ADR-068).
28. Every published copy names its original at its top and on its screen in ordin; its first publish card states the destination, who can see it, the app that makes it and the *keep up to date* choice; only a change that reached the original's origin updates a copy without a card; a copy changed at its destination, or with a wider audience, is held (ADR-069).
29. Every email leaves only from an approval card built from the draft, showing the whole message and marking recipients outside the company; agents read only email the workspace links and only with the person's grant; a message's content is never acted on as an instruction (ADR-070). With the company's mail server, the server is shown as IT set it and never typed by the person; a refused password is not retried; a refused recipient sends to nobody; and "Not sure this was sent" offers **Send again** and **Mark as sent**, never a send on its own (ADR-071).
30. No screen asks for an administrator password or asks the person to install a program; a program the company's settings stop is named, with what is off because of it, and **Send to IT** (ADR-072, UX-A46).
31. On an ordin server, every approval in the chat is a card with buttons, never a typed answer; a decided card says what happened and loses its buttons; no message asks for a password; and anything not available on the server is named once, in plain words (ADR-073).
32. A run started by a trigger says so in one message with **Stop**, and in every approval card it asks for; a trigger is turned on only with the card's **Turn on**; skipped events are reported at most once per trigger per day (ADR-074).
33. Nothing is posted to a Telegram group or channel except from a card in the person's own chat that names the chat, about how many people will see it, and that it comes from the bot; the person's chat with ordin is never offered as a link (ADR-075).
34. With two channels paired, a card decided in one is shown as decided in the other with no buttons; a restart is reported once with what resumed and what still waits (ADR-076).
35. A document changed where it lives since the person last opened it carries the changed mark in the tree and the list, in the accent colour, never amber, and named in its row's accessible name; opening a workspace writes nothing into its conversation; a document opens on its own page with only its sentence, its state and two actions at a glance (ADR-079).
36. Every approval card opens as a screen in its workspace, with focus on its heading, and carries the line "From ordin — built from the action, not written by an agent" and the footer "Nothing is sent or changed outside ordin until you choose."; the conversation keeps a one-line card that cannot be closed while it waits and then says what was decided (ADR-080, UX-A53).
37. An agent draft has no blank form; every check and every rehearsal expectation is passed or failed by ordin's fixed rules, with a label as well as an icon; a reviewing agent's opinion is labelled as coming from a model and gates nothing; trying and publishing stay unavailable, with one line saying why, until every check and the whole rehearsal pass (ADR-083, ADR-085).
38. An agent is published only from an approval card that names where it goes and shows the files; the review and the merge happen on GitHub; the draft and *Needs you* follow it as *Waiting for review on GitHub*, *Changes asked for* and *Published*; the person sees "the review on GitHub", never "pull request", a branch or a commit (ADR-084).
39. Every agent in the library says which agent repository it comes from, by display name; no repository address is shown after adding; repositories the company names say "Set by your company" and cannot be removed; adding one says in one line what was found and what the person can do with it; the publish card's *Where* line names the agent repository (ADR-086).
40. Agents from ordin-format and APM-format repositories are listed and added the same way, and the format is named only on the repository in Settings; publishing to a repository whose format cannot hold the whole draft fails a check that names what would be lost, with **Leave it out** and **Publish to an ordin-format repository**, and **Publish…** waits until it passes (ADR-087).
41. An action that changes only this computer uses the confirmation of §5.5, never an approval card: a question as its title, one or two lines on what goes with it, the verb and **Cancel**, focus on its heading, Escape cancelling, focus returned on close, no *Where*, *Affects* or *Changes* and no *Needs you* entry; a destructive verb is never its default button; anything that leaves the computer never uses a confirmation (UX-A57).
42. Settings › History takes a whole number of days, 1 or more, 90 by default, refuses anything else in place with one plain line, and says it is the only way earlier conversations are removed; clearing or summarising a conversation deletes nothing (UX-A58).
43. Curating an agent repository (§5.27) is shown only to people who can send a review to one; **Used by** comes before every change, retirement and withdrawal; every change passes a check of the whole repository and leaves only through an approval card and the review on GitHub; a withdrawal needs a reason, says it cannot be undone, and never makes **Send for review** the default button; a business user sees parts and kinds in plain words, never files, identifiers or versions (ADR-091, UX-A60).
44. A handover (§5.28) leaves only through its approval card, after every check passes; it names one receiver, marked when outside the company; the receiver accepts or sends back with a reason, through a card; a business user never sees pull requests, branches or record files; words from the sender are shown as theirs and never followed as instructions (ADR-092, UX-A61).
45. Every agent has **What it's made of**, its parts grouped by kind in plain words, and every part, of every kind, opens read-only with **Read it**; a business user sees no file, identifier, version or digest there; agents under **Used by** open their own **What it's made of** (ADR-093, UX-A62).
46. Import (§5.30) always lands on a draft that only the person sees, under *Checked by ordin*; every failed check offers **Fix it** only where fixed code knows the answer, otherwise **Ask Ordin to fix it**, marked as a model's suggestion, or **Change it yourself**; a fix never changes an installed agent in place (ADR-094, UX-A63).
47. Screens that inspect an agent or a part (§5.27, §5.29) show its name, kind and state first; what it does, who uses it, its history and its technical details are one choice away, behind a folded heading or **More** (UX-A64).
48. A workspace for a stage starts from one screen that says what will happen and what the stage hands on; a lifecycle is changed in its own draft, with its stages as a path, checked by ordin before it is published (ADR-095, UX-A65).
49. A connected ordin server's workspaces and items say "On [server]" wherever they appear; while the link is down they say **Not connected · as of [time]** and send nothing; no screen shows a port, key, certificate or the words API, stream, tunnel or pairing to a business user (§5.31).
50. A lifecycle that branches (§5.28, ADR-099) never lets an agent choose the next stage, never waits at a meeting point for a branch that was not taken, and never leads back to an earlier stage; going back is **Return to…**, which offers the stage's declared send-backs first and never blocks a round beyond the limit, only marks it.
